1. We are better off like worms. Devoid of ambition. Unified in Purpose.


The year 2000 marked the start of humanity’s Regression Era. It began on a warm Summer night in July. Police received thousands of frantic calls claiming that a strange ‘Red Mass’ approached from space. From an Earthly perspective, one could cover the Red Mass with their thumb. It was formless, constantly morphing and swirling. Many theorized that it was a flaming asteroid, a space ship, or a massive beast from outer space. Scientists were unable to classify the anomaly. It remained a news-article controversy. As months passed, the Red Mass grew in size as if it were speeding toward our planet. A year later, and it had doubled in size. And though it was perceivably larger, scientists could not discern whether the Red Mass was actually approaching Earth. Its dimensions did not adhere to human geometry.


Another year passed. The Red Mass was larger than Earth’s moon. Society panicked. Homes were boarded up. Doomsday cults swayed citizens from turbulent sidewalks. World governments prepared for the possibility of an alien invasion or an asteroid collision. Humanity was preparing for the end. Technological innovation ceased in lieu of the bleak future. Procreation ceased almost entirely. Who would bring children into a doomed world? Businesses closed and poverty ran rampant. The Red Mass was sapping the life force of humanity, sucking its blood like a vampire.


And it hadn’t even arrived yet.


2. When it rains, the worm wanders onto the road. Onto the path of danger.


Amidst the riots, a magnitude 9 earthquake decimated the western states. My home of nineteen years was reduced to rubble. My entire family died with it. My husband, daughters, and dog - all crushed beneath falling rubble. Every passing day I wish I was there with them. That a roof panel could have pierced my neck or crushed my ribs. But where was I when they were frightened, when their skulls and hearts were crushed? Convening with doomsayers. Conspiring against the government with clandestine cabals. Because I, too, was afraid of the Red Mass. I truly believed that whatever was approaching was going to swallow us whole. My mind and heart were shattered - I fell in with the rioters and anarchists. The degenerates who propelled the Regression Era.


Insurance companies were frauds. Legitimate business was scarce. My wealth and savings had vanished in bank failures. Society was consumed by the Red Mass. By the mere sight of it. A furtive insurance agency offered to house me in a motel. It was tucked in the wilderness of a far forgotten state - West Helya. I had never been to that side of the country. West Helya was the least populated state. Those who remembered it did so for the towering mountains which loomed like gods. Its main export was coal. Hillsides were dotted with mines like aerated catacombs. Often, cave-ins would crush and suffocate miners. Urban legends whispered that the wails of their ghosts could still be heard from winding mountain roads. Aside from the isolated cannibal families; the black bears and the mountain lions; West Helya imposed a curse upon the land. Sinkholes. They were as common as clandestine moonshine operations. Lives and buildings would be swallowed by the earth. What was presented to me was a life opposite to my own. An escape from screaming city sidewalks in favor of nature and convalescence. And I was so, so scared. Scared of The Red Mass, scared of myself. I could not bear the sight of my home’s crumbling buildings. The preachers and beggars. I wanted an escape from the city that had crushed my family. I wanted to surrender to change.


I embarked upon a sixteen hour drive to my motel. It was hidden in the center of the state, a region the locals referred to as ‘The Armpit’. Mountains rose taller than I could ever imagine. Their peaks grazed the Red Mass, kindling their precipices like St. Elmo’s Fire. Uncanny mammals howled from the depths of the thick woodlands. Every thirty minutes I would drive past one derelict house. Dolls, rocking chairs, and rusted metal littered their front yards. Old men sat cross legged on leaning porches, eyeing my car like predators.


I passed a small sign before my motel which read ‘Welcome to Home’. I was unable, unwilling, to recognize the unfamiliar place as ‘Home’. Yet that choice, that understanding was seemingly not of my own. The property was composed of three long buildings forming a triangle. Each building contained about nine rooms. The white paneling was smeared with black dirt and grime.  In the middle of the triangle was a dirt parking lot lined with rusted pickup trucks. A small office was nestled in the leftmost building. Within, an old woman sat in a foldable chair. Her glasses were smudged and cloudy. Without saying a word, she handed me the keys to a room as if she had foreseen my arrival, or did not care whatsoever. I was given Room 8. It was old and musty, a hundred years behind modernity. Everything smelled like an ancient cardboard box; like old pipe tobacco. It was in the far end of the rightmost building. Only one room was between mine and the woodland edge. The bedsheets, which were once perhaps white, were tainted the color of urine. Rat droppings and cockroach corpses littered the rough brown carpet. There was a small box television in the main room. But it would not turn on. I immediately noticed a head-sized hole in my wall. A direct portal to the adjacent room by the woods. Room nine. Whoever stayed there, if anyone, could spy on me. I peered through the hole. Room nine appeared dark and devoid of furnishings. A festering odor like rotting meat poured from within. Junk and fallen dry wall littered the floor. Fortunately, it appeared to be vacant. But even so, it was terrifyingly dark in there, as if blackout curtains hung over the windows.


My bathroom was disgusting and had not been cleaned. Black mold and grime stained the ceramic and tile. A dirty curtain hid the interior of my shower. It was white with small red flowers patterned across it. Around that curtain, seeping into the shower like veins was brown ooze. Utter filth, as if fungus grew on the walls. With a trembling hand, I pulled the shower curtain aside. Red graffiti desecrated the shower wall. Painted in angular letters was a list, titled:


The Men of Worms


Below the title were numbered maxims. Each bore a cryptic message about the nature of worms. They made little sense, yet there was a cryptic rhythm of poetry. A dark lullaby crawling through the ear canal, nesting and eating my brain. At the bottom of the list was a disturbing tenet. The absurdity of which rendered the entirety of the writing obscene, a fantasy. With an instinctive shudder I closed the curtain - I did not want to look at those words again.


Forming the triangle’s base was a black and sooty building, uninhabitable. It appeared to be a smoldering husk from a fire. Much of the foundation had collapsed. Windows were shattered throughout. It was an embarrassment to the neighboring structures, to the entirety of ‘Home’. A dark, mirthless parody. A few rooms had remained intact. But what sane human could have been thought to live safely inside?


3. A worm needs no material. It can be cut in half, decapitated. Yet the worm lives on.


I tossed and turned at night, sleeping atop my blanket to avoid any insects that bred beneath. There was a singular, fleeting moment of sleep. I had a dream. My husband and daughters were at the motel. They were at ‘Home’. I pictured them standing in the parking lot, holding hands as The Red Mass swirled like a vortex in the foreground. Behind them was that evil burned building. Suddenly a sinkhole opened beneath them like the gates of hell. It swallowed the left and right buildings of ‘Home’. But the burned one remained intact. I watched as my husband and daughters fell through the sinkhole, straight to the fires of the underworld.


Then I awoke to a voice in my room.


It was a skeevish, rattish voice. I lifted my head from my pillow, scanning every corner of the dark environment. That voice, whispering like a forlorn drug dealer, came directly from the large hole in my wall.


“Have you met the mouth? It only tells the truth. It can tell the future. And it will ask a great price. Go and ask the mouth.”


Behind the wall were shuffling, squirming movements. The entity repeatedly whispered in a range of languages. My body trembled with a fear I have never known. I lay still like an opossum playing dead, wishing that it would leave. Wishing that I were back with my family. Before dawn graced West Helya, the intruder sank into the dark depths of room nine. Gathering courage, I looked through the hole in my wall. I saw only rubble and darkness. A sudden emptiness flooded my heart, and I felt alone beneath the rising sun and The Red Mass beside it.


I complained to the old woman at the office. Furiously, I accused her of negligence. My eyes were red from sleep deprivation. My cheeks were wet with tears. That old woman barely flinched. I noticed then that her face may have been incapable of expression. Her lips were abnormally thin, like they had been ripped off. She was left with a perfunctory hole for breathing. Her scalp was a rotting field of greasy white curls and bald patches that exposed a flaking red scalp. 


That day, I noticed that every denizen of ‘Home’ bore a similar appearance. Balding, lipless. There were two categorical variations to their appearances. Younger men and women retained a modicum of hair, buzzed to the scalp. Their eyes were beady, and their lips were thin. The other category was scarce. Disturbing. They only left their shaded rooms at night. Their heads were completely bald. Their scalps were shriveled and red, wrinkled as if contoured to the shapes of their brains. Dark black rings hung beneath their eyelids. There were no ears to be noticed. All of them suffered from a deformity of the lips, or rather a lack thereof. What remained for a mouth was tight and puckered; hardly useful. This breed kept to themselves and their rooms. But what terrified me was that on more than one night, I saw one of those bald, lipless people crawl through a broken window of that burned building.


Outside the shelter of ‘Home’; beyond the barrier mountains of West Helya, I knew that society was in shambles. Rather than succumb to hysteria, the inhabitants of ‘Home’ lived beneath a blanket of ignorance. In my turbulent sleep, I remember hearing a motto, a cryptic phrase:


“Better to be swallowed by the Earth, than to be sucked into outer space.”


The stranger from room nine tormented me every night. I began to dread the act of sleep itself. I considered becoming nocturnal just to negate him. But I would have been left to the mercy of those night people, the lipless monstrosities congregating in the burned building.


“Have you met the mouth?” the man behind the wall whispered, leaving me to quiver like a naked butterfly. I whimpered and cried, hiding my face beneath my odorous blanket.


“Leave me alone…” I cried. “Please… Leave me alone.”


“Lost in the stars? Lost in space!? Better to be swallowed by the earth than eaten by the Red Mass.” His voice was raspy and mocking. He spoke like a man spouting rhetorical riddles as a sick game. “Ask the mouth. What is it you must know? It will tell you anything. But it will ask a great price…”


I shook violently. In my own mind, I could not ask myself the hardest questions. “Why…” I began in half-dreamt lucidity. “Why did my family have to die? Why did God spare me? Why wasn’t I killed in their place…” Sobs burst from me, and my ribs ached with guilt.


“Ask the mouth.” He replied. The man receded into the void of his room, leaving me to rot in the sludge of my consciousness.


The next morning, I peered through the hole in the wall. Pressing my face against peeling wallpaper, I could hear my own breathing. Room nine was devoid of life as it had been at all times except for the whisperings. As weak light from my room seeped into the hole, I detected subtle motion. Squirming. It was on the floor, on the walls, on the blackout curtains. All throughout was life of a malicious, infestation sort. I pulled a dusty lamp from my bedside and held it to the hole in the wall. The wire extended just far enough. What I revealed, covering every inch of the carpet and walls, were earth worms. Wriggling, crawling, thousands of slender pink bodies. Between me and an ocean of their slick, reflective bodies was a thin layer of dry wall.


In the office, I screamed. I screamed hysterically, pulling the hair from my head, crying and begging. But the old woman did not acknowledge one word. She stared straight through me as if I were a window - a statuette of glass. I thought that her attention had been captured by The Red Mass, overtaken by its malice. But as I followed her cataract stare, I looked into the emptiness of the burned building. And that woman began to mouth horrible words in a language I did not understand.


4. The worm must return to the Earth, or the worm is consumed.


It was a lonely, horrible night. My skeleton vibrated. My furniture rattled. In my nightmare, I watched as my husband and daughters were crushed by the roof we had celebrated holidays and birthdays beneath. Chunks of debris crushed their skulls. Their eyes popped out of their sockets. As if observing an x-ray, I saw their hearts gush into pools of rushing blood. And in that instant I resigned to death. I looked hopefully up at the collapsing roof, waiting for a shard to pierce me next. But every falling piece landed right beside me, missing me by inches. The ground itself collapsed around me, falling toward the molten core of the planet, leaving only a suspended platform in space atop which I stood. The Earth was shattered, and I stood alone, staring at the coagulating plasma of The Red Mass.


The quaking and trembling never stopped. I jerked my head up from my pillow, opening my eyes to my lightless room. Dust sprinkled from the popcorn ceiling above. My muddled water glasses chattered and clanked on my wooden nightstand. What was occurring was an earthquake. I fumbled for my lamp, but the light would not turn on. I stood up and raced for the light switch in my room. But the light would not turn on. ‘Home’ had lost electricity. Through the vibrations of the earth, I could hear a commotion outside. It sounded like the pattering of feet, like the marching of colossal ants. I burst through the door, into the night. The Red Mass was bright and malevolent. The night sky was burning red.


All around ‘Home’, the bald and lipless stood outside their front doors. I saw the old lady from the office in a nightgown. She stood absently, helplessly. There were those who still had their hair, who still had their lips. And there were many of those red scalped monstrosities. Like cockroaches in the walls, I had never conceived how many were hiding from me. Their gazes were fixed on the burned building. They stared lifelessly, almost fascinatedly.


At that moment, I swear the Red Mass made a noise. Like a weak heart beating and pulsing. I turned upward to look at it. The Red Mass stared back into me, larger than I had ever seen it. In my peripheral vision, the head of every lifeform surrounding ‘Home’ turned slowly toward me. Staring at me. Urging me to look back at the ground. 
The ground fluttered beneath me like it was liquefying. Suddenly the earth roared, and the opposite end of my building cracked and crumbled. I looked in time to see it sinking into the ground. A number of denizens fell into the sinkhole with the building. Disappearing, dead, never to be seen again. There were no screams to mourn them. Rather I felt that their deaths were exalted; the Men of Worms gasped and exhaled with appeasement. I fell to my knees; because once again the universe had spared me a chance at death. A chance to join my family.


Tears flooded my eyes. I turned to face the burned building. At its left end, closest to the ghastly woodland, was one intact room. Its paneling was black and sooty. The windows were lined with jagged shards of glass. A crowd of spectators gathered outside the room. A single line of them filed through the hanging door. Within, I could barely see a dim candle flickering. I noticed the piercing gazes of some, looking at me expectantly. Whether the earth still vibrated, or my knees trembled, I could not tell. A sense of doom perched over me like a raven. All at once it was me versus them. The beady eyed, red scalped men. The Men of Worms.


I slowly stood up and approached the burned building. Never had I walked so close to its blackened walls. A sulfurous, mutated odor radiated from the shattered windows. Bald, androgynous mutants glared at me from the doorway of the forbidden room. Their presence did not repel; it only invited, encouraged. Never removing their colorless eyes from my pale skin, they stood aside from the door. I crossed a threshold, a portal from the hostile hunting grounds of The Red Mass to a dusty, dark enclosure. Within, a single candle was lit on an end table. It was a lair of ashes with soot stained furniture against the walls. Three round tables surrounded a path to the bathroom. At them sat the Men of Worms. They observed me as if every movement were a test, a prophecy. I walked toward the bathroom door. A trail of black grime led within, as if a charred corpse had been dragged inside. A dim candle flickered atop the bathroom sink. I peered through the doorway and absorbed a noisome smell. It was the smell of catacombs from the world’s genesis; of gas giants and smoldering suns. The shattered toilet remained as a mound of porcelain. The tile floor was slick with wet soil and sludge. Covering the walls were meaty brown veins, like the tentacles of a squid. They ran across the wall like intricate arteries, all leading to a nucleus behind the shower curtain. The dirty, yellowed shower curtain, patterned with ornate red flowers. Behind it could be heard a soft beat, a palpitating heart. The veins twitched and writhed. Something was hiding behind that shower curtain.


“Go and ask the mouth. It only tells the truth.” A harrowing voice echoed in my mind. The room held immense pressure, and I suddenly felt I was a puppet in a blight play. Impetus compelled me, a shadowed hand guiding my shoulder, I spoke in a helpless, lost girl’s voice.
 
“What is my name?” I asked.


“Sarah Kayle” The thing in the shower responded. Its voice was deep, pervading the room as if it spoke from every angle. As my name was ushered from the shower curtain, I fell into a trance, a desperation.


“When was I born?” My breaths quickened.


“October 1st, 1969.” The Mouth responded.


Before sinking into a well of submission, I assured myself that everything said so far was surface-level knowledge. What I needed to ask was a personal question. One in which only I held the answer.


“What age was I when I ran away from home, from my mother? Why did I do it?” Between breaths I stopped - I needed more, more details. It was not enough - “Where did I hide? How long did I hide for?” 


The Mouth responded immediately. “Twelve. Your mother was a sexual predator. She threatened to kill you if you told your father. You hid in an abandoned trailer at the Lost Wood Campground on South River Road. You spent thirteen days, four hours, twenty-two minutes, and fifteen seconds in hiding.”


What should have been a flood of fear was subverted by a rush of adrenaline. Within my grasp was an otherworldly opportunity. The Genie’s lamp in a treasure trove. An omniscient crystal ball. 


“What… What is the Red Mass?” I asked quickly.


The Mouth responded immediately. “A hoax. A satellite emitting a chemical fusion of red radiance.”


I stepped backward toward the shattered toilet, the grime laden sink. “W - Why?” 


“Human Government desires to cull over ninety-nine percent of the population. An aristocracy will savor and maintain the fruits of society, as well as the sudden availability of resources and luxury. This has been in motion since the dawn of industrialization.”


My forearms tightened. I feared a heart attack was imminent. In a faint, sorrowful breath, I muttered once more: “Why?”


“Greed.” The simple response boomed, like a poltergeist had possessed the bathroom. At once, I wanted to pull the curtains from the shower - to lunge at whatever was behind, whatever lying, manipulating being. But everything it had said seemed - real. Grounded. And it knew things about me that no one should know. Suddenly I felt… curiosity. Power. Compassion for the beings and circumstances that had brought me to that room. Behind the shower curtain was truth and omniscience. A force that could have saved my family from destruction. And perhaps a wish… a wish that could bring them back.


My voice dropped to a low whisper. “Why did my family have to die… why did they have to leave me…”


“They have been reclaimed by the Earth. In service of the future, everything follows a higher plan.”


Turning toward the doorway, I ran from the bathroom with my face in my palms. Running past the Men of Worms, I stumbled. One more moment in that bathroom; one more moment before The Mouth, and I feared I would ask unspeakable questions. I would demand horrible things. Dizzy and detached, I felt like a balloon floating through the parking lot. The red sky overhead opened into a subtle drizzle, wetting the sinkhole and rubble at the far end of my building. On wet dirt, I saw an earthworm wriggling its way to the surface. Slowly rising through the soft soil, enacting a purpose I had always lacked. I rushed into my room and dove onto the bed. Thrusting my face into my pillows, I pulled the blanket over my entire body. Through the sound of pattering rain on the roof, I could hear something slimy, something gelatinous. It was surrounding my bed. Crawling through the walls.


5. Worms know the future. Therefore worms must contribute to it.


In the morning, I took a shower in lukewarm, rusted water. Clutching my body in my arms, I craved warmth but received none. Next to me were the tenets of the Men of Worms. The paint ran downward as it moistened, turning the maxims to a bloody omen. Throughout my stay at ‘Home’, I read them more than I desired. Though they pertained to worms, each red line was directly relevant. A truth in the face of my agony. And it was that acceptance, that I had come to acknowledge them as ‘truths’ that was most appalling. But from the day I had moved in and first removed the shower curtain, one tenet had remained an absurdity. A complete demolition of the code’s rationality. The very last line, the sixth tenet. Yet, if every sentence on that wall were true; if everything the mouth said were true, then the final maxim proved that there was much about this world I did not understand. I thought incessantly about the previous night. Everything had seemed surreal - nightmarish. Were it not for the sinkhole outside serving as a maddening reminder - I would have written it off as insanity.


For the first time since I arrived, I drove away from ‘Home’. With half a tank of gas remaining in my rusty, reliable car, I retreated. Alongside winding mountain roads, I was tempted with a strong urge to turn sharply. To dive nose first into the canyons and valleys of West Helya. I was starving from a lack of real nourishment. All I had eaten at ‘Home’ was indiscernible canned meat. My body was filthy from the brown water of my room’s shower. Speeding dangerously down mountain roads, I screamed and slammed my steering wheel. No matter which road I traveled, there was no escape from the future. There was no future to conceive of. Even if the Red Mass were a hoax, the world could never return to a stable civilization. My family would never come back to life. There were only two paths ahead. Both of which were equally terrifying. A frightened, inevitable death - or consumption by the Earth. 


Hunger and dizziness caused me to pull over beside a lonely convenience store. Empty cars sat in the small dirt parking lot. Many of them had been stripped of their tires and mufflers. Their windshields were shattered. On the porch of the convenience store, sitting in an inert rocking chair, was an old man with his head held down. Cautiously, I approached him. A swarm of flies stormed his scalp. Dried blood stained his long grey beard. Portions of flesh were missing from his cheeks. He smelled simultaneously of rotting meat and urine. I closed my eyes and walked past his body, entering the convenience store. Within were raided shelves and scattered belongings. My heels clinked as I shuffled bullet shells with each step. In between the fallen shelves I looked for a can of non-perishable food. I kneeled and sorted through dust for anything that I might eat. Not a single can was found, and I felt a surge of weakness as my stomach turned in on itself. It emitted a gurgling, growling sound like a rabid dog. I placed my hand over it and let out a whimper, feeling my organs rumble in pain. The gargling and growling was aggressive, sloppy. I lifted my head toward the corner of the convenience store. There lay a bathroom, the wooden door hanging ajar. I embraced my stomach, listening closely. No longer did it growl, no longer did it whine for sustenance. Something else within that bathroom had taken its place. Rumbling, it was a noise like snarling. Lightly, I pushed the bathroom door open. Under a flickering light buzzing with moths, a half naked man was kneeling over a corpse. He was tearing pieces of meat from the corpse’s stomach, feasting on them with the fervor of the impoverished. My eyes broke wide open, and I fell against the threshold of the doorway. The cannibal turned his gaze upward, revealing a mouth of missing teeth and a beard dripping with gore. Shuddering, a single grunt escaped my mouth. He looked at me, confused by my presence. Unaware of whether I was a living being or a mirage. I made no attempt at resistance. I envied the corpse before me. Gazing into each other’s eyes - four pupils equally devoid of life and purpose - the cannibal indifferently leaned over his meal, and resumed eating the flesh. I looked at the floor, at the body. My stomach growled petulantly. I recall an emptiness, a hunger which could only be described as a longing for something other than death. A surge of ambition, a desire to transcend the world the Red Mass had created.


Driving back toward ‘Home’, I thought deeply about The Men of Worms. To resist them - to deny the truths expounded by The Mouth - was a prospect perhaps unfruitful. Because I was born and raised into a world where my understanding had never been my own. The Red Mass, whether real or fake, proved not just me, but everyone to be powerless. I always wondered why people surrendered to cults. Why they imprisoned themselves beneath manipulative, coercive leaders. My mind had been groomed to walk a thin line. On one side was the life I inherited, - ruled by the government and their promise of autonomy. On the other side were The Men of Worms.


My room at ‘Home’ smelled of wet dirt. The ancient depths of a primal Earth. Surrounded by an encompassing brain fog, I sat on the floor of my shower, examining the tenets of The Men of Worms. I read them repeatedly, pondering the implications of each line.


“We are better off as worms…”


“Worms know the future. Therefore worms must contribute to it.”


I walked from my bathroom, tired, yearning for the solace of my bed. Beneath my feet I trampled the gushing bodies of earthworms. They squeezed between my toes with each step. The soles of my feet were caked with mud and dirt, and I laid them uncaringly atop my sheets. Laying amongst the worms, despite my fear of insects, was like falling into the sinkhole. Like surrendering to a roof collapsing overhead. I embraced my pillows and blanket. For the first time in my  stay at ‘Home’, I fell into an immediate deep sleep.


The man spoke through the hole in my wall. Rather than break my slumber, the raspy voice slithered into my dreams. He lulled me with his maxims, reciting them from my shower wall like scripture. He spoke them in order, starting with the first, and ending with the sixth. That sixth line - that harrowing assumption which made no more sense than the Red Mass. I dreamed that it was The Mouth speaking directly to me. Like the calm, caressing voice of a lover whispering over my shoulder. I manifested a trembling urge to rip the shower curtains away from it. To crawl into its maw and perish in whatever way it wanted.


I opened my eyes. My room was hot and sticky. I reached for my lamp but the light would not come on. Worms crawled in my bed, tickling my ankles and my feet. My arms were wet. I turned to the hole in my wall, hoping to see his eyes, hoping to hear the subtle breaths. But they were not there. I stood up and walked to the door of my room. Outside, the night was humid. In the thick woods of West Helya, animals howled and cooed. The Men of Worms did not stand outside their doors, and none stood outside the burned building. Walking barefoot across the dirt, pebbles and dust coated my wet soles. My white nightgown fluttered in a soothing breeze. I entered the burned building, the room closest to the woodland. Within was pitch darkness. Sounds like mushrooms whispering jittered in the walls. Wet, squirming earthworms coated the floor like an ocean. Moonlight beamed through the singular shattered window of the bathroom, illuminating the yellow shower curtain. It flapped softly in the night’s breeze. The wall’s veins pulsed calmly, expectantly. Pensively, as if I were that old, forgetful lady from the office, I reached out and pulled the shower curtain aside.


Covering the wall of the shower was an eyeless mouth. It was large, brown, and reflective, like a parasitic starfish clinging to black silt. It was slit vertically, inhumanly, from the top of the shower wall to the bottom. Rows of razor sharp teeth lined the vertical mouth like an eldritch shark. It pulsed and throbbed in the ray of silver moonlight. A circle of darkness surrounded it; a subterranean hole leading to atavistic depths.


Overtaking my body and brain was profound fear. The tint of the Red Mass was like blood in my vision. 


“Why should I trust you?” I asked quietly.


“You will have certainty and purpose.” The Mouth moved noiselessly. The words, mouthed by corrugated lips, echoed as if protruding from a deep cave. 


“How? You speak of certainty, but how can you be certain of anything? How can I trust you after everything in life has betrayed and abandoned me?” My voice peaked and cracked, shivered and moaned.


“You float in space with The Red Mass. You, everyone, would be better off as a worm. Unburdened by the ambiguities plaguing man. Unwavering in conviction to a higher purpose.”


As I began to cry, I thought of crawling into The Mouth, and letting my body be torn to shreds as if it were a meat grinder.


“You’re… one of those worms, spoken about in the last tenet…” I stated in sullen deference.


“Yes.” The Mouth replied immediately.


“Then tell me -” I hesitated before the brink of annihilation. The point of no return. “What is the meaning of life? What is my future?”


The Mouth twitched. The brief flicker of a smile fell upon that bathroom wall. The razor blade teeth glistened in the silver moonlight.


“Worms know the future. Therefore worms must contribute to it.” The Mouth paused. “Do you accept?”


At that moment, I could have left ‘Home’ forever. I thought of my real home, the forgotten place out west. My family. My dead family. Earthquakes. The government. The cannibal from the convenience store. The Red Mass… The Red Mass.


“Yes.” I replied to The Mouth. “I accept.”

Deep in the burned building was a room of darkness. Inside, I lay atop an old bed. The sheets were yellowed and soft, crawling with hundreds of earthworms. My body was paralyzed. I was incapable of any movement. Words were no longer capable of being produced. One by one the worms slithered onto my body. A tidal wave from the bed sheets drowning me. Reinforcements of the organ-like creatures crawled from crevices in the walls and ceiling. Tens of thousands slunk toward my body on the bed like pilgrims crawling to a shrine. From the window of that room, a singular ray of moonlight shone on me. I glistened with the sleekness of an amphibian. Worms flooded my cheeks, my toes, my arms, my eyes. Hordes of them burrowed beneath my eyelids. I felt their wetness invade my ear canals. Thousands of them slid into my mouth until my throat was clogged and my jaw broke from pressure. Beneath the skin of my scalp were rippling waves as they encircled my brain and my skull. Massive strands of hair fell from me until I was a bald cadaver. My nose and ears deflated. My lips shriveled like wilting flowers, until they were no more.


Beneath the surface of that unmoving body lay my consciousness. It floated around me, perceiving my last moments of reality through worm infested eyes. Uncertain, uncertain, uncertain - I looked through the window of moonlight at the red night sky. The Red Mass pulsed and churned. It appeared larger than ever, blotting out every star. And I thought I could see it swirling like a whirlpool. Like a black hole consuming the will of humanity. And that Red Mass did not stop growing.
