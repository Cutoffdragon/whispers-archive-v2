I keep having this horrible, recurring nightmare. It starts out as any normal day does - going to work, grocery shopping, meeting a friend for dinner. It always ends in the same place. Last night, my car would not start, just as I was preparing to travel to a relative’s. Frustrated, I considered cancelling everything - the holiday dinner, the longtime family reunion. But I had bought such an expensive gift. And grandma was nearing death every day. I called my mother, wondering if she would pick me up on her way up the coast. She had already made the trip the day prior. The call was not without its informational yield - my mother informed me that a bus travelled between my city and my relative’s town. That bus left soon - within the next hour. There was little time to think. I grabbed my luggage, the expensive gift, and hailed a cab for the bus stop.


Tapping my foot, nervous of the ominous air, I was alone at the bus stop. The entire town seemed unusual for an early afternoon. The lights of businesses were black. No feet traversed the sidewalk, and no cars drove down the street. Rising panic surged through me, though I felt real. As real as a human being inhabiting reality. The bus turned a corner, writing itself into existence with my very sight of it. I had not heard the engine in the distance, or the squealing of the brakes. Rolling to a stop at the curb, the overweight driver looked at me expectantly. His grey uniform was labeled with indiscernible lettering, unformable words. He seemingly smoked two cigarettes at once, alternately pulling each from his lips to release an exhale of gaseous fumes. I stood frozen, unable to move. Once more I thought of calling everything off. Of defying fate, and refusing to board the bus. But he stared at me - that triple chinned bus driver, sardonic and uncaring. As if the choice to board were not my own, and he knew that I would enter no matter what.


Before I could regain control of myself, I was amidst the brown leather seats. The bus was in motion, and surrounding me were depressed, brooding passengers. Each adorned a gothic, spikey attire. Their eyes lined with dark rings, hoops and spikes piercing the cheeks, the ears, the lips. They did not read, nor browse their cellphones. They just looked out the window or at the floor, as if longing for the inevitable destination. It occurred to me at this point that I had not ensured myself of the bus’ destination. I called out to the bus driver, standing up in my seat, seeking confirmation. He looked at me from the rear view mirror with black eyes, offering no response. I turned to the fellow passengers, querulously breaking questions like petulant whines. They offered no answer, nor any glance whatsoever.


The sky turned pink with sunset, then magenta - then red. An unnatural, hateful red. The clouds lingered as grey blots of smoke, manifestations of pollution. The buildings we passed were abandoned, rife with holes and destruction from a violent history. The landscape was bereft of vegetation, devoid of the color green. The ground was the yellow of rotting corn. The bus entered a single lane street, barely paved, lined with buildings that resembled taverns from an old western film. It stopped. The doors opened, and the grey, spikey passengers mulled out meagerly in a single file line. Before I could beg the bus driver for information, he turned the key from the ignition, and exited the bus himself, disappearing into one of the dark taverns as if the very building consumed his existence.


There was never any sign or word mentioning the name ‘HadesVille’, but the name pervaded my thoughts as if a suggestion of the very environment. I walked down the street, slow as my surroundings dictated, feeling the land sap my energy. Every building along the street, each derelict and ambiguous in function, was a rabbit hole with blackened internals. Only one building stood meaningful, beckoning at the end of the street. I approached this structure, a red and black tavern with swinging wooden doors. Smoke poured from the doors, and imposing men in long black coats stood at either side. I approached them timidly, asking questions, wondering when the bus would operate. They only stared in response, their heads nudging my direction within the tavern, within the smoke. I walked inside to a congregation of round wooden tables, with a singular bar at the end of the room. The lighting of the bar was red, and only red. The color of chaos, fire, and hatred. Seated at each table were criminals in black hats, black cloaks, and black boots. The color of power, greed, and death. And when red is combined with black, the only symbolic interpretation is -


Each table was too threatening to sit at. I was simply not allowed to exist within, barely feeling permission to breathe. Each face shrouded by a popped, black collar, hiding the austere expressions of gamblers, assassins, murderers. There were no backrooms to retreat - the two men from outside walked through the wooden front doors, blocking my exit. Puffing on two cigars, pointed and reflective like rifle bullets, they watched me with crossed hands. I backed away, nearly walking into tables and chairs, knowing it would be the death of me. There is only one door - one portal within the tavern. A small, solitary bathroom at the back right corner. I approach, walking past the bar tender. Heavy bags drown his eyes, red and bloodshot, vaguely staring past the walls as he fixes a drink of what appears to be blood and acid. My own skin feels pricked with needles. I dart into the bathroom - and though there is only one toilet within, it is surrounded by a stall. The walls and sink are covered by black grime. The red light within is harsh, violent in its suggestion that danger is imminent. Death is inevitable. I walk into the stall, and click the lock behind me - but upon looking again, there is no latch to secure the door. The lock does absolutely nothing.


My deep, panicked breaths become louder and louder as the background grows silent. Suddenly there is no more noise other than my breathing, the beating of my heart. The universe grows so silent, that I can hear the blood flow through my veins. The stall is a mirror dimension floating all alone in the silence of space.


Footsteps approach the stall behind me. Suddenly, I cannot turn - I cannot hear anything but my breathing, so loud and stertorous it causes my ears to ring, reality itself becoming a blaring alarm. Rusted knives pierce my back and organs as the stall door is kicked open. Someone demonic laughs behind me as I look down and see the knife stick through my stomach - brown with rust - before it slides back out, and reappears through my throat. My blood sprays the toilet, turning the black grime red. I write in the pain, screaming, shouting for help, feeling every nail pierce my skin as I am killed in HadesVille.


And then I wake up.


Another normal day, another alarm blaring at six in the morning. I resume my schedule, dressing myself in a suit and tie, taking my pills, eating my cereal. There is never a discernment of the breach from dream into reality - from reality into dream. Sometimes I fall asleep in my world, in my house. And sometimes I awake in a world where HadesVille is very real, a physical location on the planet. Somehow, I always find myself there. I always find myself stabbed, bleeding in that bathroom as the red man stabs me with his arsenal of rusted knives. Every day, whether I know I am dreaming or not, I make a conscious effort to avoid winding up in HadesVille. But some days it is simply impossible. I will drive to a familiar location, a place I know very well. Suddenly, I am lost, my heart rapidly beating as I recognize the dying grass, the red sky and black clouds above.


I’ve asked people if they know of a place called HadesVille. Some days, they say no. They look at me as if I am a madman, fantasizing about cruel places. Some days, they berate my ignorance of the place’s existence. Pointing to the very dot on yellowed maps, containing a jumbled mess of words which only my mind can piece together as HadesVille. Determined, uncaring of whether death was real, or whether I stood in reality, I drove to the spot on the map. I drove to HadesVille. In a reenactment of the inevitable fate of every man, I walk into the tavern at the end of the street. I walk past the bouncers who know that I am about to die. The patrons, the bartender who urged me on to the bathroom. And I scream and squirm in unbearable pain as rusted blades rend my eyeballs and slice the ears from my head. 


And then I wake up. Every day with the same thought as the last. When do dreams start, and what is it exactly that differentiates them from reality? Who has decided that the whole of reality is not but one dream, that we may not wake up at any point? That death is not just the end of the dream? There is much that is unknown of life - outer space, the depths of the oceans deepest trenches. And dreams, the most mysterious aspect of all. And as I put on my business suit, comb my hair, eat my cereal, I cannot bar the worst thought of all - if there is nothing before life, and nothing after death - then life is nothing at all. It simply does not exist.
