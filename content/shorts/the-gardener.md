Secret governments lurk around every corner. I came to know this when I retired from the Navy, and moved to the sullen town of Deadingham. Do not mistake the courts, the legislature, or even the president for the ruler of this world. Beneath the earth, and atop the clouds, malevolent cabals dictate the direction of the world. With gloved hands, they shape the outcome of each individual life. Once, I believed in free will. I believed that I had retired of my own will, and had moved to Deadingham because I liked the town. But  all the conversations I ever had, and all the television shows I ever watched, were subliminal currents pushing me towards predestination.


When the great plan is disturbed, granting a modicum of what this world ridicules as ‘free will’ - that is when people die. Everyone thinks they know death like they know their friends and family. But death is just another tool to the rulers of the world. No one ever commits murder - but everyone thinks that the person standing next to them is capable of it.


It all started when the boredom settled in. After working eighty hours a week for thirty years, I had grown restless. After moving to Deadingham, I found no solace in the company of its denizens. Grey, pallid faces made for dull conversation. They only spoke of the macabre - death, illness, and terrorism. The natural beauty of Deadingham was my only relief. Dark forests and verdant grass drew me to the town - or so I initially thought. I had grown too old for hiking. Local magazines depicted unique flowers and bushes one could grow in the Deadingham soil. Looking out the window of my grayed house, I envisioned an extravagant garden. I bought the necessary equipment, along with a handful of seeds, and set out to work. 


Gardening required more skill than I had anticipated. That Spring, I hardly grew a sprout. Everything seemed to wither and die as it breached the soil. Frustrated, I researched means of improving my garden. Strange websites and forums spoke of the mystical qualities of Deadingham soil. Users claimed that the finest seeds were planted deep in the ground - deeper than the foundation of homes. In a dubious attempt to contact these anonymous users, I received no reply.


Leaving the website, an odd feeling settled within me. I felt like I shouldn’t have been on that website. Like it was an illegal act. Guilty and ashamed, I constantly looked over my shoulder on my way to purchase seeds. I carried an unhinged sensation, like a puppet who had cut its own strings. I shook off the dread, determined to plant my new seeds. I spent all afternoon delving into the soil, digging deeper than graves. It was then that the tip of my shovel stabbed something. The obtrusion was hard, but penetrable. Like biting into a drumstick - solid bone surrounded by soft meat. I dropped to my knees, deep in a six foot hole. With a thick glove, I removed dirt from the object, uncovering green skin. Rotting eyes and black teeth were followed by patches of raw bone. I looked at a dead man, and he looked back at me.


With a gasp of terror, I climbed out of the hole. Through my astonishment, the world seemed unfamiliar. Still, I felt strangely loose, strangely unhinged. Pacing back and forth, I debated calling the police. But a newfound fear of authority urged me not to. I held a fantasy that I had embarked upon an unfortunate chain of events - one which was irreversible. That strange forum with secret users… how it made me feel like a criminal. I didn’t want to see the dead body again. I didn’t want to think of my home as a grave yard.


I closed my eyes, covering the dead man with dirt. He was dressed casually, wearing a red flannel and jeans. The clothing had been eaten away by worms, just as entire portions of the skin had been. Sweat stung my eyes as I covered the grave, one shovelful at a time, smoothing the dirt. What lay before me was a grassless patch. A barren reminder that I had uncovered something unspeakable. As I struggled to sleep, I could not help but watch my windows. Many times throughout the night, I thought someone was looking inwards, watching me. In a desperate attempt to block these thoughts, I imagined a beautiful garden. My unpleasant experience urged me to abandon the hobby. Determined to smite fate, I resolved to grow something before the onset of Winter.


Weeks passed before I obtained the courage to dig again. Summer had blessed Deadingham with heavy rainfall and humidity. Grass had overtaken the dead patch, blotting the grave of that poor man. I wished I had known his name. Sometimes, I thought of marking that site, and holding a private funeral for him. I doubted he had been formally laid to rest. His past was a mystery. To maintain my own sanity, it would have to remain a mystery.


I favored a section of the yard opposite from the grave, dreading to dig within thirty feet of it. Sweating from the hot summer sun, I shoveled piles of dirt over my shoulder. Drinking a cool glass of lemonade, I felt that I could truly enjoy the life of a gardener. But as I dug as deep as the forums spoke of, a familiar sensation greeted my shovel. Dead and mushy, like the cold grasp of Hades. The sound resembled crushing a bloated cockroach. And there was an awful, rotting smell. I wasted no time in dropping to my knees. Throwing dirt from the body, I uncovered a dead woman. Not as much bone showed through her sickly green skin, but she was just as cold, just as dead. Looking wildly in each direction, I slapped my face. “Wake up, Wake up, Wake up,” I muttered like a madman.


For hours, I stared at the woman’s corpse. I began to suspect that the previous owner of the home had been a serial killer. Determination overcame me as a full moon rose over Deadingham. With trembling hands, I brewed a cup of the strongest coffee known to me. Under the harrowing light of the moon, I tore at the ground with my shovel. I dug a six foot hole next to the woman’s corpse. Praying, I pleaded for no more unwanted discoveries. Then came the sound - shovel stabbing flesh. The moment the tip gouged the victim’s forehead, I wiped the dirt away, revealing the body of a young boy. To the left, I could already see another arm sticking out of the dirt. This one belonged to a young girl.


The more bodies I uncovered, the worse the smell grew. The odor was like a thousand year-old dumpster. One that had never been emptied, swallowing endless garbage and detritus for aeons. Repeating cycles of steaming in the hot Summer sun, and chilling in a Winter night. As I ran back into my house, the smell refused to desert my nostrils. I gagged, choking on the air itself. Shutting all the windows in my home, I fell ill in my bathroom. Through the retching and gagging, the smell would not go away.


The sun crept over its horizon, as my sanity sank below its own. Clutching the sides of my head, I paced next to my phone. I knew the moral thing to do - But my strings had been detached. And that awful smell tortured me, worse than any cell in an abandoned prison. I darted from my front door, quickly walking down the sidewalk. I must have looked like an escaped asylum patient - flicking my tongue, spitting on the ground with a raucous cough. I pressed my face against any bush or tree I could find, attempting to exile the odor from my mind.


Taking deep breaths, I finally came upon a beautiful patch of lavender. I worshipped it like a forsaken idol, as the languid aroma eased my mind. My head swam as my heart rate declined. I came upon Deadingham Park at the center of the town, where a solemn bench called to me. I sat down, taking deep breaths, clinging to that image of fresh lavender. Dawn brought the singing of birds, and the sound of sprinklers roaring to life. It was the final moment of peace in my life. A life that would end within the hour.


Like cancer, the smell returned. I would never know whether my tortured mind recalled it, or reality itself. But I smelled the corpses, wafting towards me from my own backyard, and the very ground beneath me. Nearby was a shed, with double doors locked by a chain. Pulling at the doors, I granted myself enough of an opening to peek inside. A sharp shovel laid within reach. Sliding my arm through the opening, I pulled the shovel from within. The metal scraped against the wood, alerting the world around me to my misdeeds. Panting like an animal, I began to dig into the earth. Just as I thought, the smell grew unbearable as I removed each foot of dirt. Early morning joggers glared at me with unease. I looked back at them with bloodshot eyes, the lower half of my face beneath the surface of the earth.


My shovel dug into the chest of a dead man. I cackled with madness, delighted that my senses had not deceived me. Kneeling in the dirt, I extracted my discovery. I held the cadaver in my arms as if it were a child. I rose from the hole and began to dig another, merely feet away. A crowd gathered, viewing me like a circus clown. They whispered with concern, recording videos of me, phoning the authorities. But when I yelled to them, imploring them to witness what their town had been hiding from them, they screamed and ran. For they too could smell the death, and they looked down into the holes I had created. 


Hours passed, and several corpses were disinterred. The sun hid behind thick clouds, pouring torrential rain upon Deadingham. Water dripped down my cheeks as I viewed my work, like an artist admiring their paintings. But there was no one to witness my deeds anymore. Deadingham had died - no people occupied the buildings around me, and no cars passed on the nearby street. As if a catastrophic storm approached, the entire town had been evacuated. 


Turning a corner down the sidewalk, a man appeared. He was wearing a black skin-suit and a red visor, completely bald and clean shaven. He approached me with concerning speed, his footsteps carrying violence and authority. I called out to him, beckoning him to witness my discoveries. His face did not shift. I turned around, and saw a man of the same appearance approaching me from the opposite direction. A dreadful thought occurred to me: the clandestine website, the town’s desertion. The removal of the strings which determined my fate. My eyes had seen that which was not to be known. I rose to my feet and ran, nearly slipping on the slick pavement. The men did not adjust their stride. They continued to follow at a pernicious pace.


I scrambled alongside buildings, trying every door I came across. Each was locked, with no lights shining within. Covered in a thick layer of dirt, I sprinted to the town hall. There, the glass doors were locked as well.  Obtaining a stone from the ground, I created my own entrance. I climbed into the dark building, clawing through broken glass. The front desk was unoccupied, and there was no electricity operating the premises. Whimpering, I turned around. The men were nowhere in sight. But I heard those strange black shoes, the heels clicking against the pavement. Worst of all, the smell within town hall was horrifying. The decay of rotting corpses was unbearable, and I believed I may die from inhaling the noxious fumes. Obtaining a chair from the lobby, I ran to a wall and smashed through the layers. I repeated this, removing chunks of wall until I came face to face with an innocent woman. Innocent with years of death and decay. Sticking my head into the dark chasm I had uncovered, I looked to my right and left. The entire building was lined with corpses, fermenting in the walls. Every direction I looked, the faces of the dead stared at me with grey, infested eyeballs. It was like peering beneath the ground, into the underworld. The air pressure shifted, as it does in the final moments of life. A life where the dead stopped decomposing decades ago. And when I pulled myself back into the town hall, removing my head from the wall, I saw a flash of lead before it pierced my forehead.

Local Navy Hero Passes away at the age of 57


On July 18th, retired Navy Captain John Palsey passed away from a sudden heart attack. John, who had recently moved to the town of Deadingham after his retirement, was found deceased in his home. John suffered from a history of heart conditions, and experienced the fatal attack as he prepared to evacuate his home in wake of the Superstorm. Though his time in Deadingham was short-lived, the town lost a great man and a hero. More on Superstorm Stanley to come.
