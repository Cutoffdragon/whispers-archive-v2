Across Route 99B, strange occurrences take place, especially in the dark of night. A destitute backroad, leading to no destination with particular haste - no rational driver would have any reason to traverse its cracking pavement. It is not a shortcut, and it is not a well paved road. Many travelers have gone missing on Route 99B. Strange vagabonds jerk their thumbs upwards, luring victims to an inconspicuous demise. Some report that the road morphs with time. Where once, there was a specific sign, or a steep curve, an unfamiliar feature would appear. To prefer Route 99B and its dark woodland thickets, is an act of rebellion against convention. Such was the case with Mike - a truck driver for a soda company named Well-Co. 


Mike detested his career almost as much as he detested people. But that was the least of his reasons for entering Route 99B at 2AM. His last eighteen-wheeler was totaled, overturned in a ditch following a road rage incident. Mike concealed the cause of the accident from Well-Co, who believed a blown tire caused the damage. Still uncomfortable in his new rig, still testing the waters, Mike had to take it easy. He wasn’t in the mood to deal with people. People, and their detest for fellow men among the road.


Route 99B was unknown to the latest generation of humanity. Some older folk remembered it in whispers and folktales. On that dark night, Mike drove his new truck down the accursed road. It was a quiet, serene drive. Coniferous pines lined the sides of the road. The bright headlights of the truck revealed unsettling figures within the wilderness. Mike saw a man in the woods - stalking, staring. Wearing a mask crafted from uncanny material. Shrugging off the paranoia, Mike turned on the radio. With a large soda in hand, he whistled jauntily, blocking any thought of what may watch from the shadows. He came to a steep curve, slowing down to accommodate the rugged pavement. Driving around the curve, he went round, and round… and never stopped. Mike was sure he had driven in a circle - twice. He wiped his eyes, and wiped his cheek. He looked through the windows, his attention consumed by the brooding woodland. All the while, sweating profusely. He never saw the deer. But he felt a thump against the truck’s grill, the rolling corpse beneath the massive wheels as the head was trampled. The bones - trampled.


The truck screeched to a halt. Mike emerged frantically from the cab, clutching a handgun. He prayed it was not a human. That it was not the man in the woods. A sigh of relief escaped him when he saw the dismembered ungulate carcass. Repulsion and disgust overcame him momentarily. The carcass was obliterated. Torn into manifold pieces, spread across the road like an unfinished puzzle. Mike revulsed, looking at the central mass of the corpse - a crushed torso, the head and neck loosely attached. If he had a shovel, he may remove the bits and pieces. Bury them in the woods. These thoughts were overridden by rage when he saw the front bumper of his truck - dented, bruised. How was he going to explain this to management? His first trip in the new company vehicle, and considerable damage had already been done. Mike jumped into the driver’s seat, slamming the door. He shifted gears, and rolled into motion. Finally, he exited the curve - the curve that seemed to roll on forever. Cursing, drinking soda furiously, Mike thought of how he would explain the incident. And perhaps a single moment was spared to think of the deer. Wondering who would remove the carcass, the intestines scattered like spaghetti. Certainly not him. Certainly not.

Streetracing. Thrilling, indulgent, and highly illegal. Tobias and Richie had been challenging each other for over a month. Two teenagers in their final year of high school, and their brand new sports cars. A pre graduation gift from their parents, unaware of their C- average. The power came with responsibility, and firm warnings from their austere fathers - no speeding, and absolutely no racing. Should they break the rules… their new rides were forfeit. An appalling thought - considering the popularity that came with the vehicles. The fake friends, and the specious girlfriends. But their banter was escalating. The idea of bragging rights became more appealing. Worst of all, everyone bet on Richie to win the race. That was a notion that Tobias could not stand. 


The competition was set in motion. All the boys needed was a hidden lane to conduct the race. Several suggestions came forth - all were too conspicuous, or too derelict. One among their clique had heard the whispers of Route 99B. The old, abandoned road that no one dared traverse. The cops didn’t bother to patrol it. It was ill maintained, and ill supervised. Notoriously long, and notoriously wild. The perfect road for an illicit street race.


Tobias and Richie met at the route’s service ramp, overgrown with shrubs. Concrete blocks, once barring access, had been tossed aside by vandals. Riding with Richie was a group of teenagers - the entire friend group, including Richie’s girlfriend. They opted for Richie’s car because he was the pick to win. He had the faster car. Tobias was alone. All seats empty but his own. No matter - Before long, they would be wishing they had ridden with Tobias. Richie’s girlfriend would be in the shotgun seat. The challengers communicated through a two-way radio. They lined up, and initiated a countdown. Three, two, one-


Engines roared, tires screeched, and smoke pervaded the voids of Route 99B - Tobias’ car a flash of orange, and Richie’s a steel blue. Metal flashed down the gray pavement, hovering over the potholes, trampling foliage beneath the wheels. Escalating from 70 miles per hour, to ninety, to over one-hundred and twenty. Everything passed in a blur. The nighttime forest. The occasional bag of garbage. The masked man watching from the woods. All of it flew by like the inexorable flow of time on fast forward. Tobias’ hands trembled, sweating as they gripped the wheel. Richie inched ahead, slowly leaving Tobias in the dust. Anger and embarrassment overcame him. Tobias could see his friends and Richie’s girlfriend looking at him through the windows. Laughing and pointing fingers. Richie, cool and confident, spared one glance toward Tobias. His white smile, the smile that had commandeered popularity in the locker room, mocked and taunted him. Richie did not see the chunks of carcass ahead. The festering, fly ridden deer. 


The wheels should have crushed the guts and the decaying bones. But they ramped off of them, sliding, burning rubber. At nearly one-hundred and forty miles per hour, Richie’s car lost control. Tobias slammed his breaks, almost ramming Richie’s car as it fishtailed uncontrollably. Richie’s friends screamed. His girlfriend clung to his arm, sobbing, as he spun the wheel rigorously. It was not enough. Beside the stretch of straight road was a steep ditch. Richie’s car dove into the ditch, nose first, speeding downward like a bullet. It crashed into a mammoth tree with titanic inertia. Richie’s head bashed into the steering wheel. It obliterated his nose, shattering his skull, and popped his eyeballs. His girlfriend was ejected through the windshield. Flying like a bird, she broke her neck and spine as she collided with the same tree. The two friends in the back seat were decapitated by their seat belts. Their blood stained the expensive leather seats. The engine combusted, engulfing the car in an inferno. Tobias came to a complete stop. His brain refused to register what he had just seen. Panicked, Tobias sprinted to the flaming car. The heat was close, searing his face, warning him away. But he could see the headless torsos in the back seat. He could see Richie slumped against the steering wheel, devoid of a face.


Tobias stumbled to the deer carcass, his eyes a great flood of tears. Before him lay an abomination. The corpse had been rotting for weeks, perhaps months. Its skin was the color of black mold, blotchy and fuzzy. Worms squirmed within the eye sockets, eating into the ligaments of the cheek and tongue. The limbs were scattered around the road, bare bone with hanging strands of flesh. Strange, mossy growth surrounded the torso. It was hairy and fetid. It smelled of pure death and decay. Tobias thought he could see a pulse underneath the bare-bone ribs. Something was beating, writhing inside. Wrought with intense nausea, Tobias sprinted for his car. Unwilling to think any more about death, he sped off down Route 99B. He sobbed like a little boy. All the while, he thought of the deer. If only the man who had killed it had cleaned up the mess - then everything could have been avoided. Richie would still be alive.


Tobias slowed down. Perhaps, if he removed the bits and pieces - if he cleaned up the stinking rot, no one else would have to die. He shuddered, pressing his foot to the pedal. Someone else would eventually do it… Certainly not him, after all that had happened. He only thought of what he was going to say to his father - If he was going to say anything at all.

Rachel’s life had hit rock bottom. She recently lost her kids in a custody war. Stolen by her garbage ex-husband. That greedy man, who had cheated on her, abandoned her, and made her look villainous throughout all of it. Her alcoholism had never been so fierce. Rachel was seeking solace in the bottle during daylight hours. It was not enough to ease the pain. Nothing seemed to nullify the anger, the rage. Instead of hard opioids or suicide, Rachel went for long drives at night. Drunk and tearful, she cared little for the outcome of her reckless decisions. She could be arrested or killed - but she didn’t care. Every night she would drive, screaming, punching the steering wheel. Come morning she would feel a sense of relief. A serenity that allowed her to sleep through the afternoon. Those nighttime drives became something of a drug. She explored different routes and unseen roads. Then she encountered Route 99B.


She didn’t drink as much that night. Her life improved as she relied less on alcohol, and more on quiet contemplation throughout her drives. Route 99B was especially beautiful, even in the darkness of night. Her headlights illuminated the ghastly essences of the forest. The full moon tinged the sky purple. She was tempted to turn off the headlights, to let the stars and fate guide her wheels. She clicked the headlights off. Truthfully, she hoped she would crash into a tree, that she would drive into a ditch -


But that desire for death and self destruction was a thing of the past. Rachel was dreaming of good thoughts. Her will was focused on self-improvement, emphasizing the soft emotions which defined her halcyon childhood. Odd shapes appeared in the woods. Movements were subtle and quick. Figures appeared eerie and human-like. The shadow of a man stood to her left, barely clear of the road. She thought she could see his thumb jutting upwards, beckoning fate for a ride. She thought she could see a strange mask. It resembled her husband in shape, as if a wraith had stolen his shadow, wearing it as a disguise. She sped forth, sufficiently bothered, re-igniting her headlights. They came on just in time to reveal the deer carcass. Rachel slowed down, gasping. A large black bird stood atop the deer, darting its gaze towards her as the headlights disturbed it. She had never seen such a scavenger before - the dark wings like an ungodly breadth of shadow and feathers. A gore covered beak beset by beady black eyes glared at her with hatred before disappearing into the night sky. 


Rachel had seen much roadkill throughout her drives. But this was no ordinary slaughter. The entire patch of road was consumed by a thick blanket of black moss. It circled the corpse like it were a tree trunk,  an ecosystem manifesting. Rachel slowed to a stop beside the nucleus. Fleshy masses invaded the orifices of the deer, with pulsing blobs of organ-substance in the eye sockets and mouth. Beneath the shattered ribs was an incubating fetus, blue with mold. Rachel wretched, covering her mouth. The fetus appeared as an animalistic hybrid, absorbing the decay of the carcass like a decomposer.


Rachel put her car into drive, retreating down Route 99B. If she never turned on her headlights - if she had not seen the inheritance of misfortune - what would have become of her? Would she have driven over it, or fallen victim to an unimaginable fate? And yet, that thing growing within... That anomaly of nature, both miraculous and far unstable. Someone would have to smite its existence.


How many drivers would encounter the carcass? How many, before the detritus of ignorance manifests into sheer evil? 


Rachel slowed down, performing a three point turn. She returned to the mass of black growth dominating the pavement. In the trunk of her car was a snow shovel. She obtained it, her headlights illuminating the massacre like a sick circus act. First, there were the bare bone limbs. Rachel scooped them with the shovel, tossing them into the thick woodland. Then, she approached the pulsing torso. The fetus within was beating and veiny. Black blood flowed within like apocalyptic rivers. Rachel lifted the shovel upwards, the sharp edge pointing down like a sword. 


Rustling leaves drew her attention. Outside the bounds of the headlights, Rachel saw the shadowy figure. The masked man. In that very moment, the mass of decay burst. A gelatinous sound, like aliens erupting from juice filled eggs ruptured the night. An abomination broke free from the carcass of the deer. Gangly limbs came into existence through rapid growth, a reversal of the rot and decay. With razor sharp teeth, the hybrid creature lunged toward Rachel’s stomach, tearing into the flesh, ingesting her organs. She screamed, dropping the shovel, and falling to the ground. The creature devoured her flesh, rapidly growing new arms and cells as Rachel turned to a carcass herself. 


But unlike Mike, unlike Tobias, that monster would leave nothing in its wake. There were no bones left of Rachel, no obstruction to the faulty pathway that was Route 99B. It clambered into the woods, moaning and clicking with a visceral roar that would be heard by none on that abandoned route.
