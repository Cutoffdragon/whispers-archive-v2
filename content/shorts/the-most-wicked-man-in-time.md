Once, I was the most wicked man in the world. A general of ruthless stratagem and prestige. Since that generation, I have watched countless ages pass like a singular heart beat. All of it, from within my prison of ice. When my army was defeated, the Arcterman navy captured me as a prisoner of war. In accordance with their customs, I was frozen within a block of ice. Arcterman voodoo preserved my cognition, leaving the rest of my body in stasis. I became a floating ghost, observing the world from a husk of deprived sensation. The first decade seemed the longest; I stood amongst many prisoners in a similar state, in the trophy room of the Arcterman emperor. Until the eventual fall of their empire, where I was salvaged by an opposing nation. The other trophies were either destroyed in battle, or discarded. They were the lucky ones. I was taken as a spoil of war. Much of the world recognized my frozen face, concrete in a guise of horror.


I wanted to scream, to ask them to end me. But the world didn’t even know I was alive. The intricacies of Arcterman voodoo were unknown to outside civilizations. I became a museum piece, floating between palaces as centuries passed. Rage filled me while civilians mocked me, pointing fingers from behind velvet ropes. Children laughed, placing their filthy palms on my block of ice. How I wished I could break free, and impale each and every one of them. But those were the better days. The days before my mind succumbed to torment.


Decades passed, one by one. I was transferred between nations - taken as a historical treasure, pirate loot, and sold for money. The hundreds of faces that ridiculed me each day became indiscernible. First, I had forgotten my name. It happened slowly - there were few days where I could remember, and many where I could not. Before long, it was lost forever, hidden within the library of forgotten mementos. My past became but a dream to me. Details were lost to time, and I couldn’t remember my triumphs in battle, or the bitter defeats that followed. I lived through those who viewed me, vicariously absorbing their memories and experiences. Until there was nothing left of me. 


The concept of language became foreign. Hearing the dialects of so many nations, morphing through the ages - it destroyed my comprehension of the spoken word. My brain became mush from a lack of activity.


Centuries passed like black voids - and this I found, was a gracious gift. I would black out for a hundred years, only to come back to myself for days at a time. Those brief interludes of consciousness were the greatest bouts of torture. Incapability would flood me. Forgotten memories whipped me like a slaver. I did not sleep anymore - I only stood in the indecipherable abyss between life and death. All the while, I could not shake the interminable terror lingering on my face. My legacy had degenerated; they called me ‘The Frozen Man of Fear’.


I did not know whether countless centuries or mere days passed before he came to me. The man with a mirthless face. He appeared amid a crowd of mouthbreathers, flashing their photographs, laughing at my misery. It was that wicked smile that pulled me from the void, into a temporary period of wakefulness. This stalker, wearing a musty black suit and a tattered tophat, stared at me. His smile stretched across his face, with unkempt yellow teeth below the lips. Unusually jagged, unlike human physiology. That stare… It felt like heat vision beaming through the ice, melting the remains of my frail brain. The way he stood unmoving, lingering as crowds came and passed… I knew. He knew - that I was alive, and in eternal suffering. How exactly he obtained this knowledge was a mystery, but no other explanation remained. 


He came to visit day after day. Always staring with the same cruel expression. Lingering for far too long. The public grew concerned. One night, as he remained past closing hours, security removed him from the museum. As they carried him away by his arms, his eyes never left mine. That smile never abated.


During the long nights that followed, I came to believe he was challenging me. He thought himself to be crueler than I was, more wicked. But he knew nothing of my suffering. I was once a terrible, terrible man. Though I could not remember my feats of destruction, hatred burned in my heart, threatening to melt the ice. But I would not see him again - not during that period of consciousness. I only knew that in the days following, something cataclysmic occurred in the world. The museum was closed. As I remained in the shadows, I could hear the bombs outside. The screams, and the death. Before long, my dreamlike haze was consumed by dormancy.


Between life sentences, I could see that man’s face. It was the only image floating in the universe, staring at me as years passed around my block of ice. I felt nothing but hate for this spectre, torturing me in the real world, and torturing me in oblivion.


Piercing red eyes pulled me back to life against my will. Burning my pupils, the world before me phased into clarity. A crowd stood in front of my exhibit, containing all the usual types. But something was different about the world, and about society itself. Everything was odd, like slight adjustments had been made to each aspect of the planet. The sky was a different shade. People wore clothes of an odd fashion, and spoke with a depressing tone. It must have been unusually long since I had surfaced. But then I saw him - still brimming with youth, as he was the last time. Standing between normal people, the stranger adorned the same black hat, grinning at me. Through my icy prison, I felt chills run along my nerves for the first time since I became an object. My slumber couldn’t have been long - he was still alive. He looked the same as before. But the world had shifted, as if he bended reality to his will.


Every day for the next week, he visited me. He stood still as a statue during the day. At night, I thought I could see him through the windows, glaring at me from a bush. One night, he broke into the museum. In the darkness, he appeared menacing. The shadows highlighted his demonic features. A cloak of malefic shade radiated around his body.


The mockery enraged me. I hoped that the goal of this man was to end me - to put me out of my misery for good. But my final remnant of pride fought against this. Somewhere, in a cosmic battle between evil forces, this person was a challenge to my greatness. The roaring berserker from my glory days perceived this as a challenge - one I could not back down from. Yet, those thoughts were futile… as every time I tried to flex my muscles, or clench my jaw, I remembered I was a mere artifact.


Days later, death blanketed the world once more. Never had I felt so afraid, not even in the face of battle. It was the uncertainty that shook my heart. Once again the world was deserted. Disaster catalyzed society. The earth quaked, and red malice overtook the sky. The walls of the museum began to crumble, and the roof caved in over my head. Finally, joy overcame me. I prayed to my gods, hoping that I would be destroyed. That I would know nothing ever again. A massive chunk of the roof fell overhead, speeding straight towards me. A moment of pure ecstasy followed. My name returned to me. My deeds of conquest and war flashed through my mind. I thought I could feel my lips move as I muttered ‘thank you’.


Right before the roof could crush me, a dark sensation overcame me. My block of ice was seized and carried away. The movements were fierce and athletic, spinning me quicker than I could anticipate. The motion blackened my vision. For the last time, I succumbed to sweet, numb ignorance.


I woke up in a hellish dungeon. Like the castle of a demon, jewels and weaponry lined the opal walls. The frost gripped me tighter. Pressure crushed my chest with the weight of a thousand pounds. I wanted to believe that I was deep beneath the earth, but I felt like the air itself had transformed. Worst of all, I was wide awake. Devoid of rational thought, I experienced only fear and helplessness. Once my eyes could focus, I saw him standing in front of me. He had been there the whole time.


In his tattered black jacket, the man approached me. Never had I tried so hard to wriggle free, to shatter my block of ice. But as he grew closer, I noticed just how deformed his features were. Like a complacent child, he sat down in front of me with crossed legs. Staring into my eyes with unblinking, red pupils. He was not going to release me from my suffering. Not now, and not ever. I tried to scream, but could emit nothing. I was his toy, a piece of entertainment in his eternal amusement.


Once, I had thought myself the most wicked man in the world. I utilized hate as a tool, smiting my enemies with rage. How wrong I had been. Never did I understand the true nature of hate. Not until the man appeared to me. It was not the hatred I felt towards the Arctermans, or towards those who made me an exhibit. True hatred was nothing like that. It had no enemies, and no logic to corroborate it. It was the product of pure entropy, destroying and torturing for sheer amusement. Now, I was the most helpless man in the world. And he was the most wicked man in time.
