Mankind relies on science to explain the world. But I should have been a mechanic if I sought understanding. My belief in science imbued me with blissful ignorance. Knowledge of mechanics would have fixed my car when it broke down in the middle of the Helyon Dead Zone. The infamous Dead Zone stretched for twenty miles, and cellphone service was null throughout every inch. Travelers were warned of the route, but a perverse few sought to shave an hour off of their road trip - myself included. The Helyons had a way of claiming those who traversed the Dead Zone. I believe mankind was never meant to tread that route at all. 


Smoke billowed from my car’s engine. The vehicle was dead. Thankfully, it was afternoon. My slim chances of rescue were greater. But if a traveler drove towards me, would they stop? Would I have stopped myself? Legends always warned us against hitchhikers and stranded vagabonds in the dead zone. Folklore told of mirages, looking to entrap any who fall for their specious plight. 


I threw my sweat-sodden towel on the ground. It was a hot day, and I was unfortunate enough to be stranded halfway through the twenty mile stretch. After two hours with no human contact, I figured I would have to walk for help. Then, from those woods which leaned in unnatural ways, I heard a sound. Not a sound of nature - a clank. Like an operating machine, or a hammer banging on metal. Wondering if humanity was nearby, I wandered off the road, and up a verdant slope.


The woods were silent. Birds did not chirp, and the wind did not blow. The sky above never waved, and the clouds never moved. Amidst that sweltering summer day, I felt as if I was locked in time. The clang sounded again, further up the mountainous slope. I hauled myself over boulders, using what footholds I could obtain in the rocky cliff. As if luring me, the clang rang out again, much closer this time. It was directly ahead - but no building of any kind was in sight. Between a pile of rocks, cluttered at the bottom of a steep mountain, was an opening. It was a cave mouth; a portal into the guts of the strange mountain. I pondered how the cave could produce such a noise. I pictured mining operations swallowed by the Helyons. Shafts operated by ghosts who never returned home. Shrugging off the delusion, I figured it was worth seeking assistance.


Daylight died at the mouth of the cave, as if it forbade the passage of light. Entering the hole with caution, I used my phone flashlight to illuminate the path. At first, it appeared to be a normal cave. But I could tell from my echoing breath that the tunnel ran deep. Deeper than any human was willing to descend. An ominous gale emanated from the depths, brushing softly against my face. Like a massive ghoul slumbered within, yawning. I listened for the clanging noise, but I was left wanting. Had the day been so hot, that I had heard altered noises in my desperation? I had only been stranded for two hours, and was well hydrated. 


As I pondered my own coherence, my flashlight went black. When I left the diner that morning, my phone had been completely charged. But it died in my hand, leaving me stranded in pitch darkness. Listening to the ethereal hum throughout the cave, I wondered if something had absorbed the battery’s charge. Cursing and slapping my phone, I began to panic. Light should have protruded from the cave mouth - I had not delved so deep yet. But those rays of sunlight were cowards, fearing whatever compelled me beneath the earth. I sat down, taking deep breaths. Rubbing the sides of my head, I devised a plan of escape. But there was no way I envisioned survival. The Helyons had consumed another traveler, luring me to a gruesome death with its false clang.


I was about to scream for help, when I heard the clang once again. I jerked my head in its purported direction. A soft, orange light emerged from around a boulder. Sighing with relief, I clambered towards the artifact of civilization. But the light abandoned me before long, leaving me without sight again. I stepped forward, placing my right foot on a jagged stone. My left foot sought solid ground, finding no such thing. I fell forward, tumbling down a steep slope. Pebbles tore at my cheeks, jabbing my back with each roll. My head slammed against the ground, turning my world into a ferris wheel. My palm was bleeding profusely. Whimpering at the sight of it, I realized - I could see my hand. I could see my body. Dim luminescence illuminated the pit. Phantasmal blue light provided just enough vision to see outlines and color. The clang was loud and repetitive now, accompanied by smaller whirs and clinks. I stood up, approaching the machine ambience. My footsteps echoed, clanking as if I were walking on metal. Like a factory, the hum of machinery dominated the environment. I approached an industrial door, built into the side of a rock wall. Puzzled, I pondered the existence of such a door at those depths.


I grabbed the handwheel, turning it to the left. Steam rushed from the door as it slid open. I entered a laboratory hallway, lined with machinery. Robotic arms worked meticulously, crafting components. The joints whined with metallic strain as the parts were placed upon conveyor belts, disappearing into the mountain’s walls. Lasers cut and shaped plastic forearms, fingers, and wrists, before placing them on the conveyor belts. I observed the machine-lined hall with childlike fascination. Walking slowly, my mouth agape, the arms worked with no regard for my existence. At the end of the hall was another door. Before opening it, I hesitated amidst brooding thoughts. What kind of place had I stumbled upon? There were no miners, and no foremen. I could only guess that I had accidentally disturbed a secret government factory. Deep in the Helyon Mountains, where few ever returned. If I ran into someone, human or not, would I survive the encounter? Was I going to be abducted and held for brutal experimentation? I swallowed, deciding that my best option was to turn around. But there was nowhere to turn back. Fate had led me to this place, and it would have to lead me out. Biting my lip, I opened the next door.


The room before me was an auditorium. Lit by ghastly phosphorescence, conveyor belts intertwined like a complex highway. Coming through the walls, plastic body parts rolled along each belt. A concave incubator augmented a neutral, grey material with lasers until it resembled wood. Similar machines mutated stone, bark, grass, and other naturally recurring materials. I approached the laser cutter, watching as it shaped atoms, transforming the texture of each grey block. The blocks were then sucked upwards through a tube - straight towards the world above. But where exactly were they going, and what was their use?


Wide windows lined the walls. I approached the closest one, peering through the dusty glass. There was a chamber containing a massive reactor. Swirling at the center, miasmal essences fused and contorted, producing a familiar clear liquid. Gallons of water flowed from the machine through tubes. My mouth hung agape, as I watched H2O being produced from thin air. 


Wiping my eyes, I walked to the next window. What I saw, laying under a surgical assembly of robot arms, was a human. With calculated precision, the arms carved eyes into the wet, clay skin. With a scalpel, it incised the opening for a mouth. Once the naked human was complete, it was rolled onto a conveyor belt, disappearing into the mountain. Glassy eyes stared lifelessly as the head succumbed to shadows.


I backed away from the window with a dizzy head. My heart beat rapidly, panicking. I wandered by a finger-creation unit. The robot arms packed the fingers in cases of ten, shipping them into the human-creation chamber. Leaning my weight against a cold, metal wall, I spotted another window across the room. In a roofless, wall-less chamber, was a model of a house. Each area of the house, and its respective material, was labeled in an unknown language. The letters were not of this world - they were unlike any hieroglyph or human language. Through the window of the house was a freshly crafted human. It stood like a statue in front of the stove, watching an abnormal television program. Attached paper tags labeled the pot in its hand, the stove, and the actions the human was performing to hold the pot. I noticed the television, rife with static, depicting two mannequins as news anchors. That was when the head slowly turned towards me.


Leaping backwards, I fell onto the metal ground. I crawled towards the nearest door, unwilling to traverse this factory any longer. But at the other side of the auditorium, something pulled my attention like a magnet. An immaculate metal door emitted a bright orange glow from underneath. Spacious ambience radiated outwards, like outer space lay behind it. My terror was replaced by a perverse sensation of curiosity. The tones of neon orange were familiar, like I had seen them at birth, or in the womb. Somehow, I thought that what laid behind the door was the truth of all mankind. The truth of creation, and the origin of the universe.


On shaking knees, I performed an imitation of walking, approaching the orange radiance. As I walked in a straight line between the manufacturers, I could feel the robotic arms turning towards me, acknowledging my presence. From the corner of my eye, I thought I saw them following me, extending from their joints - allowing me to continue forward. A static hum grew deafening, like electricity itself was being invented behind that door.


I didn’t open the door. At least, I never knew if I did. I couldn’t feel my arm lifting, or my fingers touching the ancient handwheel. But the door opened itself to me. My body floated through the doorway. I thought I could comprehend an orange brain made of stardust and binary code. It floated in a black void, with no floor beneath it. Though I hovered closer towards it, the entity never increased or decreased in size. Though it resembled a brain at first, it really took whatever shape my mind imagined. Until the last nanosecond of my life - when it became a congregation of ethereal beings, crafted from the fabric of unknown dimensions. With indigo ink, they wrote upon a neverending roll of paper. The scroll was fed onto a conveyor belt, rising through the mountain, into the world I once knew. Written in every language, both known and unknown, were the undeniable laws of science.
