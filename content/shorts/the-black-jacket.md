Highschool was wasted on skateboarding and drugs. I was at the bottom of my class, but at the top of the social hierarchy. I could do a kickflip, but I couldn’t pass a math test. In my senior year, I dropped out for good. University was a hopeless prospect, so why continue to try? My parents, angry and disappointed, pushed the idea of joining the army. But I had other plans. I was truly remarkable at the skate park - and I intended to create my own success from that talent.


My first year as a dropout was the loneliest. All my friends had gone to university and were having the times of their lives. Hearing their tales of debauchery almost made me wish I endured high school. But instead I laid in my bedroom, staring at the ceiling fan. Depressed, and unemployed. Watching it spin, and spin. I didn’t practice skateboarding anymore. My talent was consumed by drugs and alcoholism. Before long, my mother proposed an ultimatum: either I find something to do during the day, or I find somewhere else to live.


Skateboarding by myself was an unfathomable thought. But it was preferable to a mundane job, serving chicken as a fry cook. I couldn’t show up to the public skate parks alone - I had a reputation to uphold. That was when I remembered an old, private spot from my earliest days of skateboarding. Me and my childhood friends pioneered a skate park when we were thirteen. Hidden deep in the woods beside a highway service road. No one else knew about that spot; it was our secret creation. Our personal pocket of existence. We built the rails, laid concrete for a pyramid, and held bonfires regularly. I’ll never know why we abandoned that place. Eventually, everything grows old. Everything gets thrown away, left behind.


After hours of meandering along that old service road, I saw a familiar trail. Overgrown with weeds and bushes, slick with mud. With my skateboard in hand, I wandered down the tick infested path. Five minutes later, I found it; just as beautiful as the day we abandoned it. The pyramid, dirty and cracked, stood strong throughout the years. A burnt tire lay sooty and melted; a remnant of our final bonfire. Surrounded by harrowing forest, the clearing had been dominated by weeds. But the essential equipment remained: The rail, the derelict half pipe. Only one detail puzzled me. An artifact indicating that somewhere along the space time continuum, someone else had found the spot. Someone had breached my pocket of existence.


Hanging from a thick tree branch, was a black leather jacket. It was a menacing article, the sunlight dying upon its pure black pigment. I would fear whichever ruffian adorned the jacket - yet, I envied it. I walked closer, observing its qualities. Clearly, it had not been abandoned for long. It was not dirty, and there was no rain damage to the leather. The last rainfall was a week beforehand. Someone had been to the spot within the very week. Cautiously, I scanned the environment, as if I felt eyes watching me from the bushes. Only the chirping birds and the wind accompanied me. The jacket’s elegance compelled me; I turned to look at it, to admire it. I reached my hand outward - But I was far beyond wearing abandoned clothes. I wasn’t homeless yet, after all. I distracted myself, removing foliage and performing old tricks. All the while the jacket watched, swaying slightly from the tree branch. It was healthy to stretch my legs, and I knew I would come back to the spot later in the week. As the sun set, I returned home, wondering if the jacket would still be there upon my next visit.


In the days following, I was eager to return to the skate spot. The nostalgia was addictive. When I went back, it was both a surprise and a relief to see the jacket. Still black and pristine, still hanging from the tree branch. The owner had not returned to claim their lost treasure. If the jacket even had a living, breathing owner. In the darkest recesses of my mind, I imagined the jacket spawning from pure oblivion. Bound to no master; A memento from entropy itself. As I skated, the jacket pulled my eyesight like a magnet. It swayed with my movements, watching me. Following me. My desire to wear it grew greater, as its allure grew mysterious. Already I was picturing myself in the jacket, posing upon my friends’ return from college. Would I be more attractive in the jacket? Would I be more desirable?


The jacket had surely belonged to someone, and that person had found this skate spot. People often grow attached to material belongings, myself included. Perhaps they had planted the jacket as a flag, marking my skate spot as their territory. Obtaining my phone, I checked the weekly weather forecast. It was a relatively clear week, mostly overcast and grey. Torrential downpour had been predicted for Saturday, three days from the time. I couldn’t stand the thought of such a fine jacket being soiled. If its owner truly cared, they would retrieve it before the heavy rain destroyed it. I departed from the skate spot with resolve that day. If the jacket still hung by Friday, I would take it for myself.


In my dreams and nightmares, I was already wearing the jacket. I craved the malevolent image. Clean and intelligent. Bold and ruthless. I should have taken the jacket - if it was gone by Friday, then I had lost out. I would never find another jacket like that one. Such a mysterious piece of clothing. That jacket was the single sign of intrusion upon the long lost skate spot. There had been no litter, no foreign graffiti, or no signs that anyone had skated there. Just the jacket. Like someone had left it a sign. They were watching me.


Waking up Friday morning, I wasted no time. I left my mom’s house early, driving to my skate spot. Throughout the trip, I craved the jacket. I deeply wished that its owner had not returned. I wished the owner was dead. Grinding my teeth, my stomach fluttered. How badly I wanted it. How badly I wanted the jacket. And to my delight, after sprinting through the tick infested woods, I was greeted by the treasure - swinging from the branch, swaying. Waving at me. I hadn’t even brought my skateboard with me. I returned just for the jacket. Walking up to it, I sought permission to touch it, as if it were a vicious dog. I ran my fingers across the black leather. It was smooth, yet dangerous, like a venomous snake. My pupils expanded with awe, and my heart beat rapidly. Carefully removing the jacket from the branch, I held it in my hands. It was far heavier than I had anticipated. Far more than a normal jacket should have weighed. I figured something was left in the pockets - a gun, or a knife. But after carefully searching the pockets, I found them empty.


A smile graced my face. I laughed, alone and fulfilled. Finally, the black jacket was mine. I didn’t see myself coming back to the skate spot for a while. Not until I knew no one would come looking for their jacket. With haste and excitement, I thrust my right arm through the sleeve. An audible click rang out, like a button had been pressed. What followed was the greatest stinging and burning sensation I ever felt. It was excruciating, like sticking my arm in piping hot fryer grease. But it was not fire that ravaged my arm - Sharp, metal spikes jutted out from the inside of the sleeve. They pierced my arm, penetrating every centimeter of its length, down to the fingers of my hand. I uttered a gut-wrenching scream, wildly flailing my tattered limb. It was like barbed wire teeth, grinding, stinging, tearing and eating away at me. Shaking rapidly, I could not release myself as the piercing spikes bound me to the smooth black demon. Using my left arm, I grabbed the leather jacket and attempted to pull it away. With strength and adrenaline, I tore the jacket off, thrusting it onto the weeded concrete. As the jacket was ripped from my body, its spikes ripped my skin with it. I looked down in shock, hearing the drip-drop of my blood on the ground. My entire arm was bare exoskeleton, punctured throughout its length with large, festering holes.


I tried to scream for help. I tried to scream at all. But nothing would come from my mouth. My own blood, pooling at my shoes was gluttonous, indulgent. In my last glimpse of life, I watched the blood seep into the fallen jacket, like it was drinking my nutrients. The black leather absorbed the blood, unstained, immaculate. I fell to the ground and died.
