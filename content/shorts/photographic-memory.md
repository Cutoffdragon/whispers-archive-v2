Doctor Bozhurst Weizt,  PHD in Cognitive Sciences


Subject 4D awoke within a nameless lab, overseen by a nameless corporation. After surviving a horrible crash, he fell into the hands of a worse fate: those of Doctor Bozhurst Weizt. Doctor Weizt was a cruel, ruthless man. Once, he was described as keen, and curious. His domain of research resided in Cognitive sciences, with pertinence to memory function. But Weizt had been a physicist and a biologist before. Countless experiments were performed on humans and animals alike. Unspeakable breakthroughs have been achieved under the duress of Weizt’s scalpel. But the outside world would never know of them.


Subject 4D had been conveniently wiped of his identity by the recent crash. To Bozhurst Weizt, this was rather auspicious. He had a working theory pertaining to memory’s effect on the perception of reality. Subject 4D, a confused and injured man, was confined to a solid white room with no doors and no windows. Only personnel of the nameless foundation knew how to enter that room. To Subject 4D, it was as if he awoke in vacuum. He was an entity with no notion of self, floating in a room where the ceiling and floor could not be differentiated. 


Every now and then he slept. He awoke one day to a photograph on the floor. Small and flimsy, no larger than the human palm. The image was of him - Subject 4D, healthy and happy, before the crash. Forgotten memories flooded his mind. A life lost to white walls, a wife and children questioning his whereabouts. Subject 4D was overcome with joy - followed by intense misery. Perhaps it would have been better to forget the life that had been lost.


The next time he awoke, the photograph was gone. It had been extracted in his sleep through methods unknown. The pitiful subject thought little of what had been lost. From an invisible window, Bozhurst observed his experiment closely. For weeks, Subject 4D ate and drank normally. Bozhurst would converse with him over a loudspeaker. Several questions would be asked, mainly pertaining to Subject 4D’s memory of himself. Not in regards to his life experiences, but his literal idea of himself - his bodily features, the shape of his face, the color of his eyes and hair. Aside from the expected pleas for mercy and release, Bozhurst found that Subject 4D was cognizant of his own appearance. 


The subject acknowledged that he had blue eyes and brown hair. Looking at the subject, and then at the photograph, Bozhurst knew this was correct. And so concluded the first phase of the experiment. For the following months, Subject 4D was provided with humane meals and entertainment to protect his fragile mind from insanity. Any reasonable request for comfort was accommodated, with one exception - he could never have a mirror or anything containing a reflective surface. He was denied every request to look at the photograph of himself. This ate away at the subject’s contentment. He grew restless, pacing in his room, attempting to glimpse himself by any means. In a vain effort to create a reflection, Subject 4D poured his drink of water on the floor, creating a puddle. To his demise, the bright fluorescents of the room prevented liquid reflection.


Doctor Weizt was intrigued by his subject’s confusion. When Subject 4D awoke from dreamless slumber, he found his photograph neatly inserted into the room. He rejoiced, crawling to the photograph, holding it close to his chest. But there was a raising of eyebrows, a slight grimace about him as he studied the photo. Doctor Weizt smiled wickedly. Over the loudspeaker, he assured his subject that it was the same photograph as before. Subject 4D accepted the persuasion. But unease gnawed at his heart like a pestilent rodent.


He fell asleep with the photograph in his pockets, determined to never part ways with it. But it was gone when he woke up. Subject 4D spiraled into rage, throwing his room’s furniture against the walls. When Doctor Weizt questioned his insolence, Subject 4D replied: “It’s just, it’s nice to remember who you are, what you look like.”


Doctor Weizt’s lips curled into a fiendish expression of pleasure.


Subject 4D coped, eventually forgoing his desire for the photograph. A month after it had been stolen from him, Doctor Weizt appeared over the loudspeaker with questions. They were the same questions that had been asked during the first interview. When asked about the color of his eyes - Subject 4D paused. He rubbed his chin, deep in contemplation. After careful consideration, Subject 4D replied “green”. And so concluded the second phase of the experiment.


An ambiguity of self image plagued Subject 4D. He acted strangely in the following weeks. His personality had shifted, including his eating habits and entertainment preferences. Subject 4D walked with an adjusted gait, and breathed in a slightly different pattern. Every detail down to his heartbeat was monitored by the extensive technology of the containment room. 


Doctor Weizt opened his computer, browsing Subject 4D’s photograph. With editing software, he applied deformities to the image. This time, he changed more than just the eye color. He broadened the jaw line, increased the distance between the eyes, and gave the nose a slight curve. The end result resembled something almost alien, like a human with birth defects. Slight pigmentation was changed in the hair, but the eye color was untouched. A year passed before Subject 4D became restless in regards to his self-perception again. Doctor Weizt appeared over the loudspeaker, and asked the same routine questions. When asked to describe certain features of his face, Subject 4D’s replies were bland of detail. He was particularly stumped when asked about the color of his eyes.


The next day, Subject 4D leapt for the photograph instantaneously as it lay in the middle of his room. When he finally held it in his hands, his heart skipped a beat. Subject 4D flipped the photograph, and turned it around multiple times. He wiped his eyes, took deep breaths, and requested a drink of water. All day he stared at the photograph. Before him was an incredulous impression, almost unreal, but familiar enough to hold credence. The man in the photograph resembled a clay impression of himself. Doctor Weizt allowed him to carry the photograph for a week - far longer than the previous periods. Then it was ripped from him in his sleep.


Doctor Weizt recorded a significant alteration in Subject 4D’s behavior. The subject was deeply depressed, and deeply confused. He spent entire days in the corner of his room. He showed no anger towards his captors, but he certainly resented himself. He hardly requested entertainment anymore, but when he did, it was… strange television. Odd categories of websites. Even to the deranged tastes of Doctor Weizt.


No questions were asked over the loudspeaker. Rather, Subject 4D awoke to a blank sheet of paper and a handful of pencils. Politely, Subject 4D was asked to sketch itself. This came as a fearsome challenge to the subject - it was gripped with fear upon receiving the task. Apprehensive to pick up the pencil, Subject 4D paced about its room, unsure of how to proceed. Twenty-four hours passed, and not a single line was drawn. Shaking and trembling, Subject 4D declared that it was refusing the task. Doctor Weizt did not like this, not at all. In the days following, Subject 4D received no food or water. As it shriveled into an emaciated skeleton, Subject 4D crawled to the pencil and paper, which had never left its room. With closed eyes, it drew itself. Witnessing what it had created, it retched in disgust, retreating to the room’s corner.


When Doctor Weizt observed the drawing, he cackled villainously. The depiction was inhuman. A ghoul with an oblong head, gangly limbs, and spasmodic pupils. The jaw line was contorted, and the hair flung wild in every direction. Folding his hands in and out of each other, Doctor Weizt went back into the photo editor, and annihilated the photograph with edits. He giggled and snorted, shifting excitedly in his seat. His nameless colleagues grew fearful of the Doctor’s machinations. Whispers spread throughout the nameless laboratory that Doctor Weizt was amid powerful and disturbing work.


Time flowed irregularly for Subject 4D. By the time it received the final photograph, it had no conception of months, weeks, or years. For all it knew, it had been but a day since the accident. It shrieked upon waking up, seeing the photograph in the center of the room. It scrambled back to the corner of the wall, hiding its face with trembling arms. Doctor Weizt appeared over the loudspeaker. “Look at it.” He said with booming authority. Subject 4D pleaded, begging for the photograph to disappear. “Look at it.” Doctor Weizt said once more.


Subject 4D faltered under the Doctor’s tone. To deny the cruel man’s will was to succumb to torture far worse than death. Subject 4D crawled toward the photo, the paper flapping atop a ghostly draft. It examined their hands, legs, and feet as it crawled - bewildered, as if they belonged to another body. Subject 4D picked up the photograph between two fingers. Tears ran down its oblong, alien cheeks. From an unseen room, Doctor Weizt shook with anticipation. His excitement could not be contained; he appeared over the loudspeaker. “This is what you look like.”


Subject 4D took a deep breath, and forced their eyes open. One split second of vision, one miniscule glance of the photograph - Then the subject shrieked wildly, tearing the photo to pieces, scattering each shred across the room. Doctor Weizt, brimming with laughter, leaned into his microphone. “Now tell me, what do you look like?”


Subject 4D was unresponsive - For the rest of that day, and the days to follow. Doctor Weizt rigorously observed the subject’s behavior, which had become… animalistic. It seemed unable to utter any known language, their speech degenerating into mumbles and clicks. It clawed at the white, impenetrable walls. It picked at its own skin, consuming it despite no lack of available food.


Subject 4D had become like all of Weizt’s subjects: a mindless cadaver. It was at this point that Doctor Weizt’s experiments would typically come to a forced conclusion. He stared contemptuously at his subject, who had been rocking silently in the corner of the white room. A human vegetable, mindless roadkill. The subject should have been euthanized at that moment. But a wicked imp poked the Doctor’s brain. He had not forsaken the experiment yet - there was one last observation to be made.


A strange, whirring noise emerged from the center of the subject’s room. Chewing on the tips of its fingers - its bloody, skinless fingers - it slowly turned to see an object covered by a black blanket.


“Hello Subject.” Came the ominous, booming voice. “Surely you do not recognize me. But today, I have given you what you always wanted. What you had always asked for in the beginning.”


Subject 4D groaned, licking its fingers like savory meat. On hands and feet devoid of exterior skin, the beast approached the strange object. It was like an ancient monolith deposited by a cruel god.


“You always wanted to see yourself in the mirror. Go ahead, look underneath the cover.” Said Doctor Weizt.


A brief shard of consciousness flashed through Subject 4D. For the last time, it contemplated its identity. But the only image that remained was the demonic imprint. The wight it had become. It had to know - it had to be certain. Fear paralyzed the nerves, as it slowly removed the drape from the mirror, and looked at its true appearance.


A blood-curtling scream filled the entirety of the nameless foundation, and Subject 4D began tearing the skin from its own face.
