The artist named Sandor ruined my life. Every day since he left me has been a slope to hell. I no longer took interest in anything, save for plotting a twisted revenge. I remember the first day I ever met him - I was a meek paparazzi girl, no real name to my tag, flashing pictures like the rest of them. Sandor strolled along a golden path, his beautiful eyes obscured by sunglasses. The enigmatic persona chilled me. I knew then and there that I wanted to see those eyes, to know their color. Repeated confrontations gave me my chance. While I will not disclose the details of our relationship, know that Sandor turned out to be like every other millionaire. He was keeping concubines, a status which I was reduced to. Many of the other women were fine with this, fully aware that they were one among many. I was certainly not. 


My rage broke the specious relationship, though I never lost sight of Sandor. Mostly because I never truly came to know him. Sandor haunted me, marauding my dreams with possibilities of what could have been. A life in an ivory mansion, sulking in a lavender bedspread. No, it never occurred to me how obsessed I was until we split off, and I obsessed over the idea of revenge. How badly I wanted to reduce him to rubble, to put him lower than the pit in which he discarded his women. I was already dreaming of slanderous news articles causing Sandor’s reputation to diminish. But in the depths of my hateful mind, I came to realize that it would never be enough.


Sandor was not your typical actor. He was neither just a painter or a writer - he was a jack-of-all-trades when it came to the arts. He called his works ‘aesthetispheres’ - a conglomerate theme of paintings, writings, and plays that gave way to something far more greater than just a single work could do. He composed the music, wrote the scripts, and derived everything from his own mind. I knew that Sandor cared little for fame and the money. It was his pursuit of passion that motivated him. His career could be destroyed, his reputation thrown in the trash, but he would never lose enjoyment in that passion. So really, my revenge had to dig far deeper, to hit Sandor at his molten core. I wanted to destroy him from the inside out - I wanted to destroy that passion.


Depravity drove me to irrational thoughts. I devised plans to burn his completed works, though I knew he kept backups of everything. I thought of poisoning just enough of his brain, to the point that it no longer worked correctly, and could no longer produce art. I dreamed of tying him down and cutting off each limb, so that he may never paint, write, or walk ever again. Of course each of these options would land me in prison for life. And I wasn’t willing to sacrifice my life for Sandor, not after so many women had. There had to be a perfect solution. That solution presented itself when Sandor fired his agent Charlie.


Charlie held a similar resentment towards Sandor. He had been his agent for almost his entire career, and was fired simply because Sandor thought he ‘didn’t fit the aesthetic’ anymore. So you can imagine that in a way, Charlie was also bent on revenge. Just not in the insane way I was. Either way, I used my connections to arrange a secret meeting with him. One where we would devise Sandor’s doom. He recognized me right off the bat, and knew perfectly well what I sought to discuss. At first I feared that Charlie would only be after Sandor’s money - but he was even more heart stricken than I imagined. 


“Sandor’s lost his mind.” Charlie started. “He’s no longer cool and collected. He’s become withdrawn, a loner - ever since he started talking about his greatest aesthetisphere yet.”


“Hasn’t he always been broody?” I reminded Charlie. But the look on his face told me that Sandor’s depression was of a different nature. Sandor dove face first into his work, and whatever he was working on was of a dark complexity. Sandor was known for his sullen, yet existentially beautiful work, like a painting of a girl being absorbed by the whole of the galaxy.


“Sandor’s lost interest in the more… congenial aspects of life.” Charlie muttered, with sleep deprivation in his eyes. “He’s become obsessed with horror, absorbing art and literature from the most nihilistic minds of our time. Whatever he’s working on is going to reflect this to a far deeper extent than me and you can imagine. I mean, in truth, I just don’t think the man has it in him.”


“These things always made him truly sad… something’s changed in him.” I said condescendingly.


“Yea, but listen!” Charlie held revelation on the tip of his tongue. “He’s fully committed to this. It’s like he’s throwing out his entire life to begin this new piece. When he fired me, he looked like he hadn’t slept in days. The luxurious villa, the one you used to stay at with him, well he donated it! That entire beautiful estate… and he didn’t even keep a penny from it. Now, he’s taken up residence in some massive gateau, the old abandoned mansion that used to belong to one of those nihilistic artists I mentioned…” Charlie swallowed. His chest was filled with hurt and betrayal. 


“You seem worried for him.” I confessed. “You’re aware that we’re here to discuss his downfall, yes?”


“Yes, of course.” Charlie wiped sweat from his head. “It’s just, I think the man’s creating his own demise. He was always such a soft soul, now he’s living in some dark, dank mansion in the middle of the woods! I mean, how’s he gonna sleep at night? He’s afraid of ghosts!”


Ghosts, dark mansion - a perfect idea formed in my head. “Charlie, how big is this mansion? How dark are we talking?”


“Oh, it’s the worst! Big as the president’s place - it was actually a preserved historical site, but Sandor used his influence to take it for himself. And it’s completely isolated, there’s not a single street that leads to the mansion. You have to walk a backwoods trail for twenty minutes just to get a glimpse of it.”


Charlie showed me a picture of the mansion on his smart phone. The home was lined with dark, rotting wood, beset by a sullen pine forest. And he had not been lying - it was the biggest house I had ever seen. Smiling slowly, I licked my lips with sadism. Charlie had always been afraid of not just ghosts, but demons, the undead, and just the dark in general. I remember how he shivered under the blankets, worrying that some apparition was waving to him from the closet. Yes, the plan was perfect in my head now - and Charlie had already served his part. He needed no more information from me.


I stood up abruptly, and stuck out my hand. “Oh, are we done?” Charlie asked, surprised.


“Yea, thanks for the information. I’ll let you know if I think of something…” But indeed, the idea had already culminated.


Once, I had interviewed a famous makeup artist, particularly known for her macabre imagination. She had transfigured many of the villains in Sandor’s films, able to make a kind old gentleman look like the nastiest zombie one had ever seen. She was renowned across the world, and I still had her contact information.


We set up an appointment, providing her with as little information as possible. Upon meeting, I gave her the most vague instructions: “I need to look dead, drowned, utterly phantasmic. I’ll let you take full creative control, as you do it better than anyone, but know this - when you are done, you should truly ask yourself whether the woman before you is a blighted ghoul, or a drowned victim of the underworld.”


The makeup artist set out to work. I was her complete product, and I was not disappointed with the outcome. She turned me around in the chair, and pointed me at the mirror. My once red hair was now jet black. The luscious curls had become ragged wisps, flinging in each direction. The best effect was how she darkened my eyes, making them look sunken and malevolent. She applied contact lenses turning the pupils dull and grey, the color of a muddy lake. Wrinkles beset my cheeks and lips, turning my face gaunt. She even did her best to make me look emaciated with her body work. The result was truly perfect - I looked like a malicious poltergeist, a banshee.


All that was left was to find the location of the mansion. Charlie’s cue that this place was a preserved site had made it all too easy. The mansion was called ‘The Ulter Hell.’. Once owned by the famous writer Garrett Cull, the mansion had been abandoned for over fifty years. Nonetheless, the address was still posted plainly online. I noticed that there had been no information posted about Sandor’s relocation. He had kept it a secret. Apparently the Ulter Hell had been a favorite amongst paranormal hunters and delinquents alike. Surely, Sandor would have some security measures in place to prevent trespassing. Then again, perhaps he would consider the unwelcome guests part of the authentic experience. Soon enough, he would have one more guest - one who sought to drive him mad.


My bag was packed lightly, containing only the bare hygiene essentials. I had no clue how long I would end up squatting in the Ulter Hell, but I knew food would not be a problem. When Sandor didn’t occupy the kitchen, I would bandit food. If he noticed that some steak had gone missing, well, that would just contribute to the haunting scenario. The Ulter Hell was large enough for ten families to live within it, and never see each other. If I desired, I could stalk Sandor from within his own walls for the rest of my life.


The taxi drove me to a desolate road. “Ma’am, are you certain this is where you want me to drop you? There’s nothing here!” The driver looked back at me dubiously, as we parked along the side of the wilderness. “And what are you doing dressed up like that? Halloween was last month you know.”


Grabbing the driver’s hand, I slipped two hundred dollars into his palm, closing his fingers around it. “Yes, this is exactly where I want to be dropped off. And yes, I’m aware that Halloween was last month. Now you take that gift there, and be sure you never tell anyone that you saw me.” I stepped out of the car - as soon as my two feet hit the grass, the cab screeched outward, disappearing into the mist with frantic speed.


The path to The Ulter Hell was overgrown. I struggled to find the trail, but upon discovering it I embarked with a wicked grin. Deep, dark clouds blanketed the sky overhead, leaving the woods rather dark for noon. I even heard an owl amongst crows, as if permanent night blanketed this land. I will admit, the pine forest was scenic. Each tree was evergreen; dark yet beautiful. In my recent years of depression, I had come to appreciate such things. Apparently, Sandor had as well.


After walking for about thirty minutes, the mansion stood in the distance like an opal monument. It sat about a large clearing, a circle all too perfect for these malefic woods. After approaching, I crouched behind a tree, scanning the area for signs of security, and Sandor himself. Yet, the Ulter Hell looked just as abandoned as it had in the old pictures. There were no cameras, no vehicles to indicate that a human inhabited this place. Even some of the windows were broken. Graffiti tattooed sections of the rotting wood. I began to wonder if Charlie had deceived me, setting me on a wild goose chase. Nobody could be believed to live in that haunt - not a single light was emitted from within. Still, I thought of Sandor, and the kind of man he was. This was reason enough to believe the contrary - to inhabit this place for the sake of art, was not out of bounds for Sandor. Quite the contrary.


A window on the ground floor lay shattered, granting me my portal. After sprinting through the front yard on light feet, I lifted myself through the window without much noise. I wouldn’t want to be completely noiseless during my stay - just not noisy enough to indicate that another living human was here. The room I entered was a den. Papers were flung in every direction, scattered across the floor and the red velvet couch. The room smelled like noxious must, making me feel faint. Crouching behind the couch, I listened for any sign of Sandor.


The Ulter Hell continuously emitted its own rendition of white noise. Not the soothing ambience that people sleep to, but rather an unsettling, ambiguous whine. Like the mansion was saying hello, acknowledging the newcomer’s presence. Depositing my bag and sneakers behind the couch, I wandered to the next room - a foyer. My tattered banshee robes fluttered with an unseen draft as I admired the spacious abode. Staircases lined each side, with dusty paintings ascending the side of the stairs. These paintings were strange - each one contained a normal human from an old, Victorian era. However, there was something uncanny about the expressions on their faces. Each person was grimacing slightly, barely wincing. Only noticeable on a subliminal level.


Shrugging off the oddities, I realized that this foyer was one of many. The Ulter Hell was a maze in itself - I came to wish that a map had existed of the labyrinth. Walking heel to toe, I wandered restlessly through the halls of the second floor. I began to affect my gait to resemble a lost girl. Like a lobotomy patient wandering lost through the insane asylum. If Sandor were to come across me, there would be no mistake - I was a visage from his past, coming to haunt him.


As the day passed into night, my search became frustrating. Searching for this quiet man was like looking for a corpse in a dump. As I entered one end of the mansion, Sandor could be transferring to the other. How hilarious it would have been, if I had never found him once during my stay. But as I came across a study, I finally found my first real evidence that someone, particularly a brooding intellectual, was inhabiting these walls. The study was lined with bookshelves, the ceiling uncomfortably low. Like a firefly in a bottle, the fireplace was still smoldering. He had been here sometime today. How incautious Sandor was, not caring if this mansion burned to the ground. Just how low in his esteem had the artist become?


Laying as an open book on the desk, was a journal by Garrett Cull, titled ‘The End of This Eternal Midnight’ . It was not a published work, but a handwritten journal. The yellowed pages contained annotations in fresh ink, likely Sandor himself. 


‘Cull detailing the mystery of his own disappearance?’ wrote Sandor next to a passage detailing Cull’s isolation and disgust for humanity. Peering in between the lines, I sat in the cushioned chair and flipped through the immediate pages. The journal was a confession from Cull, stating that he had been dead in the eyes of the public for years. His body was never found. Myths entailed that Cull had died in his own mansion, which swallowed his corpse, as if it were some sort of living beast itself. The journal was written in such a way that it was intended for someone to find and read it, long after Cull’s disappearance.


The floor creaked softly nearby. My body shivered with anticipation. Simultaneously, I felt afraid. Here I was, playing the part of the banshee, fearing that otherworldly entities may not welcome my presence here. The approaching foot falls could be Sandor, Cull, or something infernal. As my mind ran sprints, I closed the book. Sandor would notice that it was not opened to the same page. I hesitated to open it back up, realizing that this would instill paranormal unease. If it were indeed Sandor who approached, I wanted to toy with him before revealing myself. So I crouched underneath the desk, hiding from whatever presence entered the study. 


A body entered the room. With light feet, it walked to the desk, where it paused. I was terrified at that moment - how fruitless this endeavor was becoming. To be Sandor, and to live here by himself; I couldn’t imagine what his next aesthetisphere was going to entail.


Silence ensued for minutes, until I finally heard that sweet voice. “Batsheva?”


It was his voice, Sandor’s voice. Either that, or something impersonating him. The call was soft and compassionate, but Batsheva was not my name. Likely the name of some other ephemeral concubine he had kept. The thought of him calling some other woman enraged me, and I wanted to leap from under the desk and strangle him at that very moment. However, I restrained myself. 


Skin brushed against the leather cover of Cull’s journal. Sandor moaned with ghastly undulance, an intimate moan. I shivered upon hearing such a noise. Then, as quietly as he had come, Sandor walked out from the room. I had no intention of losing his trail. To my advantage, the hallway was pitch dark. From a distance, I followed the man who walked in the hall. It was still too dark to tell, but I swore it was Sandor. The shape of his body was there, but everything else was not, as if he had been possessed. At every moment, I prepared for him to turn around and see me. I would stand still, not moving a muscle, letting him know that someone was here. That someone was watching him. And he would come to know it was not this ‘Batsheva’.


One step fell a bit too loudly, causing the floor to creak. Sandor stopped walking, but did not turn his head. It was as if he knew he was being followed, but was too afraid to confirm this. He emitted that familiar moan. “Batsheva?” He whispered. Again, I trembled with rage, as Sandor stood needlessly quiet for minutes. Then, like a machine jolting back to life, he continued his stride.


Sandor flicked on the light of a massive dining room. From the hallway, I stood peering from the darkness, watching his every move. And finally, I could see him. It was actually him, the artist Sandor. How long it had been since I saw those eyes. How much I had not realized I needed that man. But his appearance was disheveled. Once there had been an artist clad in jewelry and fine style. This haggard misanthrope wearing a tattered night gown was not that man. His hair was as frizzy as my banshee hair, hanging down to his shoulders. A thick beard covered his neck, and he was awfully pale. Upon finally seeing him for the first time in years, I felt sympathy at his obvious troubles. Instinctively I wanted to drop everything - to run to his arms, apologize for my insolence, and beg him to take me back. But I bit my tongue and held firm. These were weak, irrational thoughts. They were exactly what Sandor would intend for me to think.


Absent mindedly, Sandor walked to the stove and poured cold tea into a dirty glass. He took the smallest sip before placing the glass down, and walking to the dining table. Before him was an open journal, beset by a quill and inkpot. Sandor sat before this journal without writing in it, and without even looking at it. He just stared forwards, unblinking. This ensued for at least ten minutes, before with sudden rigor, he obtained the quill and dropped his face to the journal in one fell swoop. With the speed of adrenaline, Sandor wrote furiously in the journal. I could see the veins in his hand pulse as he gripped the quill. He moaned and groaned as he wrote, panting and whimpering, becoming louder and louder as his face nearly touched the page itself. The whimpers became querulous, bordering screams of shrill anxiety.


I stepped backwards, once again creaking the floor. Sandor jerked his head upward, dropping the quill. “Batsheva?!” he yelled with a nervous voice. I stood as still as ice, barely glimpsing half of Sandor’s face. Sandor jerked up from his seat. His knee banged against the dining table, knocking the inkpot over and blotting his own work. Like lightning, Sandor darted from the dining room down a dark hallway opposite me.


“Damn!” I said to myself, not wanting to lose sight of him. Still, I did not want to run. My plans required pernicious patience to erode his mind. If I had to lose sight of him a few times, so be it. I would search the ends of the earth for him, just to make him feel as I once had.


The open journal left me curious, and I could not resist a peek. Much of the current page had been covered by ink, and it had seeped through a portion of the journal. Obtaining the first page, I read Sandor’s current work, his first contribution to the aesthetisphere. Skimming through the first chapter, I read a detailed backstory of a young woman named Batsheva. She was a demure romantic, always seeking love at every corner of the autumn. I stuck my tongue out at the pages, unwilling to read more of this woman’s tragic origin. Quickly I flipped to the most recent chapter, much of which had been lost to the ink. What I was able to read was grotesque in detail. The chapter entailed Batsheva’s death and torture at the hands of a deranged captor. The characteristics of this captor were that of Sandor himself. It was his most profane writing yet. He spoke of dismembering her body, performing experiments on her bowels, and unspeakable acts committed on her remains. The journal trembled in my hand as I realized what Sandor had become.


I closed the journal, placing it back on the table, before entering the hallway Sandor had disappeared into. The hall was long and dark, diverging into many other hallways and rooms. I began to fear I would never find the man; until I literally almost ran into him. He stood like a statue, staring into a room, which was just as devoid of light as the hall. My body was close to his now, and he must have detected my physical presence. Sandor was in a trance however, staring ahead of him, as if glimpsing the void. Absently, he wandered into this room, flicking the light switch. As soon as luminance gave way, Sandor moaned and whimpered, verging on panic. From around the corner, I could not understand what he was looking at. Soon, I would come to know.


As he whispered to himself, Sandor walked out of the room. He repeated incantations, such as “There, I did it…” and “They aren’t here, they aren’t there…”, before he merged back into the shadows of the hallway. Unable to resist, I peered into the room. The light had been left on, making it look like the only real location atop a world of pure shadow. As I walked in, I glimpsed canvases scattered throughout the room. There must have been about twenty. Some of them were on easels, some were on the wall, and some just lay scattered on the floor. Each contained a brutal painting of a human under extreme torture. They all covered a different topic of subjectivity, but it seemed that no stone was left unturned. The colors of each painting were old and dull, with one exception - the red was far too bright, almost crimson. As if real blood had been used to create these images.


Some of the paintings induced nausea from their graphic detail. It was too much, even for my dark mind. Covering my mouth, I retreated from the room, kneeling outside the door. I wanted to forget that I had ever seen those things, to forget that room existed. But they stood right behind me, the paintings staring at the back of my head.


At this point, I no longer knew if my ordeal was worth it. I thought of calling off the operation, retrieving my few belongings, and retreating from The Ulter Hell. This whole experience would be choked up as one crazy night.


A distant scream ruptured my train of thought. Sandor wailed with such pain and emotion down the hall. My legs shaking, I stood up and walked towards the source. I felt that my actions were no longer my own, that I had committed to a course of events that was irreversible.


Thunder boomed throughout the mansion. Strangely, the sound seemed contained, as if one of these dark rooms contained a storm, rather than the outside world. Flashes of lightning briefly illuminated the dark hall for split seconds at a time - but there were no windows. During each flash, my mind hallucinated, showing me more deranged paintings of bloodied victims. An angry man with an axe in his skull glowered at me from a canvas, blood dripping down his scarlet beard. There was a statue which looked too life-like, a human impaled by a pike. Everything hammered down upon me, and I began to run, ignoring the visions of people wearing pig heads and swollen masks.


I ended up in a larger foyer, which contained massive double doors. From within came whispers, like a chorus of spirits in congregation. Peering through the open space, I witnessed a bedroom, dimly phosphorescent from forces unknown.  The bedroom was massive and opulent. At the center was a canopy covering the bed itself, which was far wider than anyone would ever need. I thought that Sandor would lay in the bed, chatting to himself, convulsing over his own depraved thoughts. But from the corner of the bedroom, I saw his rocking body, crouched into a ball. He rocked back and forth, muttering incessantly, clutching his head.


It was hard not to feel pity for this man. How far he had fallen from the confident persona he once held. What I had come here to do had perhaps taken place already. Sandor had already gone insane. He did not enjoy creating this aesthetisphere; he was a victim of it. As for what the remainder of his work entailed, I did not care to know.


The reasonable thing to do would have been to leave at that moment. I had gotten what I came for, a glimpse of Sandor suffering from his own delusions, crying and nearly wetting himself. That alone should have satisfied me - had perverse nature not stabbed my stomach. Sandor had abandoned me. He had left me alone. How badly I sought to kick him while he was down. Just once, I would reveal myself to him, here and now. I would show myself to be a haunter from his past, and truly push him over the edge.


“Sssssandor….” I whispered in a low, hissing tone. “Ssssandooooor….”


He stopped shaking and rocking. Releasing his temple from his own clutch, he slowly lifted his head. Then, the longest moan of all came forth, slow and painful.


“Ooooooooooohaaeeeeeeeeeehhhhhh….”


I winced at this guttural exhalation. It was rigor mortis from a dying man.


“Sandor…” I continued uncomfortably. “Do you remember me?”


Slowly, like a possessed puppet, Sandor turned his head to face me. His eyes were wide, and his mouth was turned downward.


“I have come back Sandoooor…. I’ve come back to haunt you….”


“Eheh,” Sandor nervously giggled. His expression contorted, and his body began to quake with each laugh. My brow furled in exasperation. He had no right to laugh here and now - he was supposed to be exposed and worthless.


Approaching Sandor, I knelt down before him, ensuring he could see my pale face, and my malicious eyes. “You remember me, don’t you?” I asked, almost hopeful. “Go on Sandor… say my name…”


Sandor uttered a mix of nervous laughter and whimpers. “So… so then it’s you… it’s real…”


“Yes, say my name…” I demanded bitterly.


“It’s you… Batsheva…” 


“No!” I yelled. I dug my fingernails into his knee, baring my fangs at him. “Look at me, say my name! Tell me you remember me, after all you had done to me! Say it!”


But instead, Sandor began to rock again, ignoring the spikes penetrating his knee, as if he felt no pain at all. “It’s true… it’s real… it happened…” Sandor repeated similar phrases over and over. He had forgotten me. With my face this close to his, even behind the costume makeup, he should have recognized me. All the years we spent together, and he had forgotten my face. I wanted to hurt him. I wanted to kill him. The satisfaction I had come upon prematurely was not enough anymore.


Sandor gripped my hand. It was cold to the touch, and clammy with sweat. His lips dropped into a stuporous gape as he rose to his feet. Like a mummy, he wandered off aimlessly again. Through my agony, I knew that Sandor was not in that skull. For my sake, he must not have been. This was a brainless patient, lost in eternal limbo. 


“Yes, yes, it’s real…” Sandor mumbled, staggering as if he were drunk. I stood up and followed him, digging into my own palms with my fingernails. In a sense, I was just as shattered as Sandor. My emotions vied for dominance in a hurricane. My heart could not settle on complacency, nor hatred, nor sorrow. I could only follow along, while I debated internally on abandonment, or murder. But a deeper desire clawed to the surface. The Ulter Hell was a dungeon containing secrets. At the bottom, I would come to know Sandor as he had known himself. I needed to know why the artist had fallen so far.


Sandor’s silhouette was swallowed by the hallway. I followed the only sound I could: his wicked laughter. Thunder continued to ring through the mansion. In the brief flashes of lightning, I no longer witnessed the same halls. The wooden floors had become red, and wet, as if it was made of human flesh. The moment I crossed into an unknown gateway hit me like a nightmare. It was crossing into another world, and suddenly I knew I was not in the mansion anymore. I would like to think that I began to descend into a basement of sorts, or perhaps an attic - but I felt that I was still walking straight, on level ground. Ground that was made of clouds and tendrils.


Along the hall, I heard more moans, and more whimpers. Not of just Sandor, but of many suffering souls. Sandor’s laughter died down to mixed wails of amusement and agony. I ran my trembling hand across the closest wall, and I felt bars. Metal bars, like prison cells lined the wall next to me. A finger reached out to touch mine. I could feel the distinct texture of corrugated flesh pressed against my palm. Jerking my hand back, my heart palpitated in my chest. This was a true place of darkness, devoid of all light, where vision was impossible. I had provided myself with no means of guiding my way. I no longer knew which way was forward, and which was backward. Sandor’s voice seemed to come from every direction at once. The halls seemed to be closing in on me, growing tighter, trapping me like vermin. 


Some of the prisoner’s cries resembled humans, and some resembled hybrid voices - humans merged with animals. A flash of lightning gave me a glimpse of sharp fangs behind rusted metal masks. I will never forget those faces. The flesh had been torn from their cheeks and foreheads. Their eyes had been replaced by barbed wire and metal straps. I wanted to scream, but at every nanosecond, I felt like someone was breathing down my neck, waiting for me to make a sudden movement, so that it could pounce upon me. Behind me, I pictured Sandor, a ghoul as depraved as his prisoners, with artistic lust burning in his eyes. 


A spotlight illuminated a stage before me. As my eyes averted themselves from the flesh caged humans in the light, I noticed that I was no longer closed in between prison cells. Unsettlingly, I could not see any walls whatsoever. I was floating in a sphere of oblivion with no floor, and no roof. There was only the wooden stage before me, floating with no regard for physics. On the stage were the exoskeletons of humans. Their eyes were intact, containing vibrant blues, greens, and browns, as if they had been real once.


They squirmed, writhing in agony, attempting to extricate themselves from barbed wire, which constricted them like a statue. Each one looked at me, bare of skin, save for those watery eyes, the last container of their individuality. There was no explanation for this cruel act; I needed a mode of escape. The Ulter Hell provided me with none at the moment, for I could not discern where the halls began, where they ended, and whether I stood upon physical ground. Beginning to walk away from the stage in a hopeless direction, I felt I may fall off a ledge and sink endlessly as the stage shrunk before me. Sandor’s laughter had died out, leaving only inane mumbles to serve as the ambience. Each whisper echoed through my eardrums, like a gunshot in an enclosed container.


The whole act of reality had ended. I surmised that I may truly die within the Ulter Hell, as I saw no way that a sane human could withstand these horrors. Only one image drove my consciousness - Sandor himself. He was the only eidetic picture I could think of, the only waypoint on the lineless map. Walking endlessly into the void, I began to weep. Truthfully, part of me had felt that this was a long time coming. The horrors had numbed me, and my consciousness resigned to death. Sandor had killed me from the day he used me like a piece of silverware, and threw me in the trash after he was done eating. I had spent life entirely in rumination since then, plotting a device which would serve me no salvation. What I was served here was the perfect ending of sorts, a just desert. I only wished that I would have been able to live a life apart from Sandor, a life where I created my own art, and my own meaning.


Flashes throughout the darkness revealed a physical miasma, coagulating and swirling about me, as if I wandered through the clouds. I held my head downward, my neck craning like an old crone, as my skin deteriorated with the environment around me.


Thump.


My forehead banged into a hard wooden door. I felt the door knob, and the bars covering a small porthole at the top of the door. It was a medieval furnishing, one that led to forgotten pits and executioner’s chambers. Behind those bars the faintest red glow was emitted, flickering and flaming. I turned the rusted knob slowly, and pulled the heavy door open with a slow, agonizing creak.


This led to a hallway lined with stone and torches. The flames were unnaturally red, as if this were the very depths of hell itself. Bubbles popped loudly as I traversed the path, and the heat grew unbearable. Smears of blood ran across the stone, and piano music chimed softly from a chamber ahead. The melody was unspeakably sad, with soft notes that chimed at disparate intervals.


Sandor sat at his piano on a stone bridge in the middle of this chamber. Alongside the bridge was a moat of bubbling lava, crimson in hue. I was drawn to the piano like a bird to the flock, the perfection lulling my steps. It occurred to me that Sandor’s fate was out of my hands. The environment dictated  finality - once again Sandor would have his way with me, even if it were no longer his soul that composed the chorus. Sandor’s head jerked backwards, looking at me, as his fingers continued to press keys in rhythm. 


“You lied…” He quivered. “You are not my Batsheva…”


His hair was ragged, and his scalp shone with sweat through bald patches. His bed robe was tattered now, exposing parts of his emaciated torso beneath. Then, rapt with fascination , I viewed the painting in front of the piano. Words could not describe the portrait, for language would defeat its purpose. With bright reds and opal black, the painted woman held herself between heaven and hell. I had read Sandor’s journal, the story of Batsheva, and I knew that this depiction was her. I began to tear up before the ornate frame, large enough to cage a monster, floating in the air. It was amazing and horrible, the greatest achievement of mankind, as well as its apocalypse.


“Sandor…” I muttered. “Why? Why would you make something like this?” Batsheva stared at me from the portrait. It contained an entire world, like looking through an interdimensional portal. “Why would you paint something like this? Why would you do this to the world, to the human mind?”


Sandor began to play the piano faster, granting danger to the atmosphere.


“You don’t know what it was like to be me, you don’t know what I have done to myself.” Sandor’s head turned unnaturally on the neck, almost peering directly behind him like an owl. “My art has destroyed me. I had explored every corner of life and meaning through literature, music, and pastel. I had seen the highest elevations of heaven, but not the lowest depths of hell. The true artist must explore the universe from each perspective - he is not limited to his planet and its worldly meanings.”


A man behind me wrapped his arms under my shoulders, holding me in place. My struggle was futile, as he possessed the strength of the undead. When I turned my head to look at him, I breathed in his fumigated odor. He was ancient, as old as a vampire, with grey hair and skin that contoured perfectly to the shape of bone. As Sandor looked at this man, they both nodded at each other, like they were old friends. Lifting himself from the piano bench, Sandor produced a knife from his waist. Viciously he approached me, clutching the handle, fondling the blade.


Pressing my body against the man behind me, I squirmed. “No…” I begged. “No!”


Sandor dug the blade into my stomach, quickly drawing it from my body. It entered with the anger of a depressed man seeking meaning. It exited with the excruciating pain of one who realized the world was null.  The sharp blade ruptured my organs, driving a shot of adrenaline into my skull. As my eyes turned downward, and my knees grew weak, Sandor walked towards the painting of Batsheva. My head grew dizzy, and the world spun on a swivel. Sandor delicately obtained a paint brush, and dabbed it on the blade of the knife, which contained the color of my blood. Using a small step ladder, he rose up to Batsheva’s eyes, which were hollow. But with the scarlett hue of my blood, Sandor brought life to those sockets. He gifted Batsheva the most menacing pupils, flaming red and filled with contempt for humanity. 


My body functions ceased as I took in the completed masterpiece. What I saw next was a vision granted by the thin line of death. It ripped me to a void between worlds, as the center of the painting began to protrude. Something lay beneath the surface of the canvas, and began to rise up at its center, seeking to break free from its prison. Sandor stepped down from his ladder, looking wildly between me and the painting. For the last time, he uttered that horrible moan, filled with the fears of a man who was about to witness the destruction of many lives. Sandor fell in a void between satisfaction and regret. Before him stood a miracle carrying irreversible consequences.


In my grips of death, I almost understood Sandor’s dreams and depravity. As the ego died, I could finally see through Sandor’s lens. The painting of Batsheva was truly a masterpiece, but it was not meant for the minds of warm humans. To truly comprehend it as I did at that moment, one would have to have a fatal wound, and taste their own blood.


The piano music continued to scale upward, despite no active orchestration of the instrument. The bump from Batsheva’s stomach grew larger until it burst, and a clawed hand dug its way from the painting. The gargoyle hand was followed by a long, grey arm, giving way to that face - the malicious face of Batsheva. My captor dropped me in my own pool of blood.Even though my body died, my brain was still witnessing the horror before me. The massive form of Batsheva stretched its way into our world, extricating itself from the painting, which had served as her prison.


Sandor and the mysterious man held their arms outward, embracing her in full,their moans rising to a crescendo as the rest of her nude body exited the portal, followed by gangly legs, and feet with jagged claws.
